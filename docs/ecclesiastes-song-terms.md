# Ecclesiastes and Song of Songs: recurring terms and book-wide choices

**Status: approved by the founder (2026-10-16), with every suggestion below
adopted.** Nothing in either book was drafted when it was approved. These are the choices each chapter will be drafted from. Read
with `docs/english-style.md`, which wins where the two differ, and with the
Proverbs and Psalms already published, whose wording both books reuse where
they share it. The founder's rulings on the questions this page first left open are
listed at the end.

## Ecclesiastes

| Hebrew | Literal | Balanced | Readable |
|---|---|---|---|
| *hevel* | breath | a breath | like a breath / nothing lasts |
| *havel havalim* (1:2; 12:8) | Breath of breaths | Breath upon breath | Nothing lasts! Everything passes like a breath |
| *qohelet* | Qoheleth | the Teacher | the Teacher |
| *tachat hashemesh* | under the sun | under the sun | here on earth / in this world |
| *re'ut ruach* / *ra'yon ruach* | a chasing of wind | chasing the wind | like trying to catch the wind |
| *yitron* | gain | gain / what is gained | what good it does |
| *'amal* | toil | hard work | hard work |
| *chelek* | portion | share | what life gives you |
| *'olam* (3:11) | eternity | a sense of eternity | a longing for what lasts forever (uncertain decision) |
| *yir'at 'elohim* / "fear God" (3:14; 5:6; 12:13) | fear God | fear God | honor God with awe (as Proverbs 1:7) |
| *kesil* | fool | fool | foolish person |
| *ra'ah* | evil / misfortune, as the sense needs | | |

- ***Hevel*.** The book's key word, about 38 times. Its picture is a breath
  or vapor: something real but brief, which cannot be grasped. The
  published books already render it as "breath" (Psalms 39:6; 144:4; Job
  7:16), "a vapor" (Proverbs 31:30) or "a puff of air" at Readable. This page
  keeps the picture at Literal and Balanced, so that "everything is breath"
  can carry each of its senses: fleeting, beyond understanding, futile. A
  main meaning decision at 1:2 gives the range: brief, elusive, empty and,
  for some readers, absurd. Each later use has a word_sense decision where
  the sense leans one way. The traditional "vanity" and the common
  "meaningless" are named as alternatives. Readable says what passes like a breath,
  keeping the picture ("Nothing lasts! Everything passes like a breath";
  elsewhere "like a breath", "does not last", or "cannot be grasped", as the
  sense needs, with the word_sense decision).
- **Qoheleth.** The word means one who gathers or addresses an assembly. At
  Literal it is kept as a name. Balanced and Readable say "the Teacher",
  with a decision at 1:1 naming "the Preacher" and "the Speaker" as
  alternatives. 1:1 and 1:12 present the speaker as a son of David, king in
  Jerusalem. A culture decision says the book never names Solomon, and that
  readers differ on whether the voice is Solomon himself or a persona.
- **Voices.** 1:1–2 and 12:8–14 speak about Qoheleth in the third person
  (the frame); the rest is his own first-person voice. Neither takes
  quotation marks. A decision at 12:9 marks the change. 7:27 ("says
  Qoheleth") is noted where it interrupts.
- **Prose and poetry.** Most of the book is reflective prose. The poems and
  the clusters of proverbs are set in lines where the Hebrew accents and
  parallelism mark verse: 1:4–11; 3:1–8; 7:1–14; 10:1–20 in part; 11:1–6;
  12:1–7. The poem on old age (12:1–7) gets a meaning decision on its
  images (the house, the grinders, the silver cord), each footnoted where
  readers differ.
- **Contradictions.** The book sets sayings against each other (for example
  2:13–16 against 2:24; 7:3 against 9:7). The translation does not smooth
  them. A theology decision at 1:2 says the tension belongs to the book.
- **Versification.** Hebrew 4:17 is English 5:1, and Hebrew 5:1–19 is
  English 5:2–20. A footnoted decision at 4:17 and 5:1 says so.
- **Parallels.** Words shared with Proverbs reuse its published wording.
  Examples: "the fear of the LORD"; *kesil*; the sluggard's folded hands,
  4:5 against Proverbs 6:10.

## Song of Songs

| Hebrew | Literal | Balanced | Readable |
|---|---|---|---|
| *shir hashirim* (1:1) | The Song of Songs | The Song of Songs, the greatest of songs | The most beautiful of songs |
| *'asher lishlomoh* (1:1) | which [is] Solomon's | by or about Solomon (decision) | |
| *dodi* | my beloved | my beloved | my love |
| *ra'yati* | my companion | my darling | my darling |
| *dodim* (1:2; 4:10; 7:13) | lovemaking | love | love |
| *'achoti kallah* (4:9–5:1) | my sister, bride | my sister, my bride | my bride, dear as a sister (idiom decision) |
| *benot yerushalayim* | daughters of Jerusalem | women of Jerusalem | women of Jerusalem |
| the adjuration (2:7; 3:5; 8:4) | one wording at each level for all three | | |
| *shalhevetyah* (8:6) | a flame of {{divine_name:the LORD|Yah}} | a mighty flame | a blazing fire (uncertain decision) |

- **Speakers.** The Hebrew names no speakers, but the gender of its verbs
  and pronouns shows most changes: the woman, the man, the women of
  Jerusalem. The renderings add no labels, which are not in the source. A
  meaning decision at the first record of each speech names the speaker.
  Where the speaker is unclear it is marked uncertain and footnoted (for
  example 1:4b; 5:9; 8:5a). Speech takes no quotation marks, as in Psalms,
  except speech quoted inside a speech (5:2; 3:3). Section headings may name the speaker where the
  grammar fixes who speaks ("She longs for him"), and not where it is
  uncertain.
- **Frankness.** The Song is love poetry, often sensual. Literal and
  Balanced keep each image (the breasts like fawns, the garden, the
  fountain), so the poem keeps its metaphors and its reserve. Readable may
  explain an image where it would otherwise be lost: a culture decision
  says what it meant. It never makes the poem more explicit than the Hebrew
  is, and never more coy. The images of the body in 4:1–7, 5:10–16 and
  7:2–10 get culture decisions on what each comparison meant. This follows
  the register rule set for Ezekiel 16 and 23.
- **How the Song has been read.** A theology decision at 1:1, footnoted,
  covers the history of reading. Jewish tradition reads the Song as God's
  love for Israel. Christian tradition reads it as Christ's love for the
  Church or the soul. Many modern readers read it as a poem about human
  love. The renderings follow the plain sense of the poem. They add no
  `deity_pronoun` spans and no allegorical wording.
- **The divine name.** The Song never names God, except possibly in
  *shalhevetyah* (8:6), which ends in the short form *Yah*. At Literal it
  is written "a flame of {{divine_name:the LORD|Yah}}", as Psalm 118:5
  writes *Yah*. Balanced and Readable read it as an intensive ("a mighty
  flame"). A footnoted, uncertain word_sense decision gives both readings.
- **Plants, spices and places.** Henna, nard, myrrh, Sharon, Gilead, Tirzah,
  Lebanon and the rest keep their names at every level. A culture decision
  explains each one at its first use, where it helps. Units do not arise.
- **Versification.** Hebrew 7:1 is English 6:13, and Hebrew 7:2–14 is
  English 7:1–13. A footnoted decision at 7:1 and 7:2 says so.
- **Poetry.** The whole Song is set in lines, following the accents.

## The founder's rulings (2026-10-16)

1. ***Hevel* at Readable:** "Nothing lasts! Everything passes like a breath"
   at 1:2 and 12:8; elsewhere the breath picture with the sense the context
   needs. Not "meaningless".
2. **Qoheleth:** kept as a name at Literal; "the Teacher" at Balanced and
   Readable.
3. **Speakers in the Song:** headings may name the speaker where the grammar
   fixes who speaks, and not where it is uncertain.
4. **The Song's title:** "Song of Songs" names the book everywhere; 1:1 at
   Balanced and Readable spells out the superlative.
