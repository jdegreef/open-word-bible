# Joshua, Judges and Ruth in French, Spanish, Swahili, Vietnamese and Korean

**Status: proposed, awaiting native reviewers.** These are the choices used to
draft Joshua, Judges and Ruth in five languages at once, so that chapter
blocks drafted in parallel read as one translation in each language. They
continue the earlier notes, which still hold and are not repeated here:

- French, Spanish and Swahili: `docs/pentateuch-fr-es-sw-terms.md` (names of
  God, recurring terms, names, measures, narrative tenses, reference Bibles
  not to reproduce) and each language's style guide.
- Vietnamese and Korean: `docs/genesis-vietnamese-korean-terms.md` (names of
  God, recurring terms, names, address between people, measures) and each
  language's style guide.
- All five: `docs/psalms-proverbs-five-languages-terms.md` for *ḥesed*,
  Sheol, *ʿeved* and the other words shared with Psalms.
- The English choices for these books: `docs/numbers-ruth-terms.md`.

If a choice here changes, every passage drafted under it is revised.
Decision text stays in English, quoting the words of the language.

## All five languages

- Translate from the Hebrew, using the literal gloss, the English and the
  meaning decisions to see how a sentence was read. Never from a reference
  Bible. Joshua 1, 24:15, Judges 6–7, 16 and Ruth 1:16–17 pull hardest:
  draft from the gloss and rewrite any line that matches a well-known
  version for more than a short common phrase.
- **Lines follow the English record**: only Judges 5, Jotham's fable
  (9:8–15), Samson's riddles and sayings (14:14, 18; 15:16; 16:23–24),
  Joshua 6:26 and 10:12–13 are set in lines (`\q`), at the levels where the
  English has them.
- **Lists of names** (Joshua 12–21; Judges 1) keep every name, in the
  language's usual form; Readable adds "the town of" only where the English
  Readable does.
- **Speech** takes the quotation marks of each language's narrative (the
  Pentateuch drafts for French, Spanish and Swahili; double quotes for
  Vietnamese and Korean, as Genesis).
- Rendering decisions: zero to two per sentence, only where the language had
  to choose. A book-wide choice is recorded once in each block (its first
  use); later uses follow it.

## Language by language

- **French, Spanish, Swahili**: narrative tenses as the Pentateuch note
  (French passé simple at L and B, passé composé at R; Spanish preterite;
  Swahili *-li-* then *-ka-*); *ʿeved* as the Pentateuch note ("Moses the
  servant of the LORD" is *serviteur* / *siervo* / *mtumishi* at every
  level, as in Psalms).
- **Vietnamese**: narration as Genesis; address forms as the Genesis note
  (brothers by age; husband *anh*, wife *em*; master *ta*, servant *tôi*).
  Ruth and Naomi: Naomi says *mẹ* and *con*, Ruth *con* and *mẹ*; Boaz says
  *ta* and *con* to Ruth at first (2:8), she *tôi* and *ông*.
- **Korean**: narration in the plain written style; speech levels as the
  Genesis note. Naomi speaks 해라체 to Ruth and Orpah; they answer in
  해요체 or 합쇼체 with 어머님. Boaz speaks 해라체 to Ruth and his reapers;
  Ruth answers in 합쇼체.

## Settled while drafting

AI-draft choices recorded so later work follows them; reviewers may change
any of them.

- **"Servant of the LORD"** (Moses, Joshua): "servant" at every level in all
  five languages (*serviteur*, *siervo*, *mtumishi*, *đầy tớ*, 종), where
  the English Literal says "slave"; literal slavery (9:23) stays "slave".
- **Seas**: Salt Sea at Literal, Dead Sea at Readable (French *mer Salée* /
  *mer Morte*; Spanish *mar Salado* / *mar Muerto*; Swahili *Bahari ya
  Chumvi* (L, B) / *Bahari Mfu* (R), as Deuteronomy 3:17; Vietnamese *Biển
  Muối* / *Biển Chết*; Korean 염해 / 사해); the Great Sea is Literal and
  Balanced, the Mediterranean at Readable (Swahili *Bahari Kuu*).
- **French** town names: *Kiryath-* (as the Pentateuch), *Kiryath-Yearim*,
  *Ayyalon*, *Barak* (Hebrews 11:32).
- **Vietnamese**: "serve" God or the gods is *phụng sự* (Joshua 24 and
  Judges); *shofet* is *quan xét* (L, B, as Acts 13:20), *thủ lĩnh* (R).
- **Korean**: inheritance is 유산 (L, B); speech levels between characters
  follow the Genesis note, with 하게체 for an older man to a younger and
  하오체 between equals in tension.
  Narrative endings and the Judges verdict formula now follow the Kings note:
  했다 (not 하였다) at B and R, and 주의 눈에 / 주 보시기에 / 주께서 악하다고
  하신 일을 했다 (L / B / R); the table's 하였다 forms stand at Literal only.
- **Numbers**: figures where the English has figures; Korean spells numbers
  out in Hangul.

## Before review

Only rendering decisions were added; the meaning decisions are the English
records' own. Reviewers should look first at the decisions marked uncertain,
the address forms between characters, and the lists of names.
