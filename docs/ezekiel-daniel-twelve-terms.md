# Ezekiel, Daniel and the Twelve: recurring terms and book-wide choices

**Status: approved by the founder (2026-10-15), with "son of man" kept at
every level.** Nothing in these books was drafted when it was approved. This page sets the choices that every chapter of Ezekiel,
Daniel and the twelve Minor Prophets (Hosea to Malachi) will be drafted
from, so that chapters drafted in parallel agree with each other and with the
prophets already translated. If a choice changes after drafting, every
chapter drafted under it is revised. Read with `docs/english-style.md`, which
wins where the two differ, and with `docs/isaiah-terms.md` and
`docs/jeremiah-lamentations-terms.md`, whose tables apply here unless this
page says otherwise. The founder's rulings on the questions this page first
left open are listed at the end.

## Carried over from Isaiah and Jeremiah

These hold unchanged:

- the divine name, always a `divine_name` span;
- "the Lord {{divine_name:GOD|Yahweh}}" for *Adonai YHWH* at every level;
- "the LORD of hosts" / "the LORD of Armies";
- the prophetic formulas *ne'um YHWH* and *koh 'amar YHWH*;
- *hoy*, *massa'*, *bat-tsiyyon*, Sheol, *hesed*, *torah*, *mashiach*, the
  Chaldeans and *'oy*;
- "the word of the LORD came to me, saying", from the Jeremiah table;
- the vocative {{divine_name:LORD|Yahweh}} with no "O";
- Isaiah's speech rule: the prophet's poetry and God's first-person speech
  take no quotation marks, speech quoted inside an oracle takes double quotes,
  and the narratives quote speech as prose does.

These books also share four rules with the earlier prophets:

- **"The day of the LORD"** (Joel 1:15; 2:1, 11; 3:4; 4:14; Amos 5:18, 20;
  Obadiah 15; Zephaniah 1:7, 14; Malachi 3:23) is "the day of
  {{divine_name:the LORD|Yahweh}}" at every level, as Isaiah 13:6. The main
  note is at Joel 1:15, and Amos 5:18 notes that Amos turns the hoped-for day
  against Israel.
- **Parallel passages reuse the wording already published** wherever the
  Hebrew is the same, with an allusion decision for each difference:
  - Micah 4:1–3 reuses Isaiah 2:2–4.
  - Micah 3:12 reuses Jeremiah 26:18.
  - Obadiah 1–9 reuses Jeremiah 49:9–16.
  - Joel 2:13, Jonah 4:2 and Nahum 1:3 echo Exodus 34:6–7; they reuse its
    words where they quote it and say where they change it.
  - Nahum 2:1 reuses Isaiah 52:7 (*mebasser*).
- **New Testament quotations.** The Old Testament text is translated from the
  Hebrew or Aramaic, not brought into line with the New Testament's Greek. An
  allusion decision names the quotation and, where the wording differs, says
  that the New Testament follows the Greek (Septuagint) or adapts the text.
  The cases below are listed under each book.
- **The Branch** (*tsemach*, Zechariah 3:8; 6:12) is capitalized at every
  level, as Jeremiah 23:5. "The angel of the LORD" follows Genesis 16:7.

- **"Son of man".** The phrase means "human being". God uses it to address
  Ezekiel, and it stresses that he is a mortal before God. Ezekiel is a
  specific man, so it takes no `gender` span (style guide §6). Every level,
  Readable included, keeps "son of man". A footnoted meaning decision at 2:1
  says what it means, and adds that this is not the title Jesus
  uses for himself in the Gospels. Later uses refer back to 2:1.
- **The hedged descriptions in chapters 1, 8 and 10** ("something like",
  "the appearance of the likeness of") are deliberate: Ezekiel will not say
  that he saw God directly. Every level keeps a hedge. A meaning decision at
  1:28 explains them, and Balanced and Readable may shorten repeated hedges
  but never drop them.
- **Ruach in 37:1–14.** The passage plays on the word's three senses: wind,
  breath and Spirit. A footnoted word_sense decision at 37:5 names the play,
  and each verse states the sense it chooses.
- **Chapters 16 and 23.** These chapters picture Jerusalem and Samaria as
  unfaithful wives, in explicit sexual language. Literal follows the Hebrew.
  Balanced and Readable state the meaning plainly: they neither blur it nor
  make it cruder than the Hebrew. The Hebrew verb *zanah* follows the
  published books: "prostitute oneself" (Literal and Balanced) and "be
  unfaithful, like a woman with many lovers" (Readable), as in Judges 2:17
  and Jeremiah 3:1. A register decision at 16:1 and 23:1 marks both chapters
  for careful human review. Oholah and Oholibah (23:4) keep their names at
  every level, with a footnote on their meaning ("her tent", "my tent is in
  her").
- **The king of Tyre (28:11–19).** The poem uses Eden imagery. A footnoted
  theology decision says that later readers applied it to Satan's fall, and
  that the text addresses the king of Tyre. This follows Isaiah 14:12.
- **Gog (38–39).** In 38:2 and 39:1, *nesi' rosh meshekh wetuval* is "the
  chief prince of Meshech and Tubal" at every level. An uncertain, footnoted
  word_sense decision gives the other reading, "the prince of Rosh, Meshech
  and Tubal", and says that *Rosh* is not Russia. Revelation 20:8 gets an
  allusion decision.
- **The temple vision (40–48): measures.** In 40:5 the measuring cubit is "a
  cubit and a handbreadth", a long cubit of about 21 inches, so the reed is
  about 10 1/2 feet. Literal keeps the units. Balanced and Readable use
  `units` spans worked out on the long cubit throughout 40–48 and 43:13–17,
  for example `{{units:about 87 1/2 feet|fifty cubits}}`. A culture decision
  at 40:5 explains the long cubit. The span's alternative writes the number
  as the style guide says: in words up to ninety-nine.
- **The temple vision: architecture.** Many building terms are uncertain:
  *'ulam* (portico or vestibule), *'elim* (jambs or pillars), *ta'* (alcove or
  guardroom) and *gizrah* (courtyard or separate area). Each one gets an
  uncertain word_sense decision at its first use, and later chapters reuse
  the same English.
- **The 500 "reeds" of 42:16–20.** A footnoted text_variant decision notes
  that the Septuagint reads "cubits".
- **Offerings in 45–46.** A hin is `{{units:about a gallon|a hin}}`, as
  published. 45:11 makes the ephah and the bath the same size, so in Ezekiel
  both are `{{units:about 6 gallons|an ephah}}` /
  `{{units:about 6 gallons|a bath}}`, and a homer or kor is ten of them
  (`{{units:about 60 gallons|a homer}}` for liquids,
  `{{units:about 6 bushels|a homer}}` for grain). A culture decision at 45:11
  says the estimates vary and that the published books give an ephah as about
  20 quarts.
- **The opening date (1:1–3).** "The thirtieth year" is uncertain. A
  footnoted culture decision gives the usual readings (Ezekiel's age, or
  another reckoning), and the record dates the vision to Jehoiachin's fifth
  year of exile, 593 BC.
- **Ezekiel 4:5 and 4:9.** The Septuagint reads 190 days, not 390. A
  text_variant decision notes it.
- **New Testament allusions:**
  - chapter 34 and John 10 (the good shepherd);
  - 36:25–27 and John 3:5;
  - 37:27 and 2 Corinthians 6:16;
  - chapter 1 and Revelation 4;
  - 3:1–3 and Revelation 10:9–10;
  - 47:1–12 and Revelation 22:1–2;
  - 40–48 and Revelation 21.
- **Prose and poetry.** Most of Ezekiel is prose. The laments and the oracles
  set as poetry are set in lines (`\q`) where the accents and parallelism
  mark verse: 19; 26:17–18; 27:3b–36; 28:12b–19; 31:2b–18 in part; 32:2–16.
  Each poetic section has a decision at its start.
- **Versification.** Hebrew 21:1–5 is English 20:45–49, and Hebrew 21:6–37 is
  English 21:1–32. A footnoted decision at 21:1 and 21:6 says so.

| *mashiach nagid* (9:25) | an anointed one, a prince | an anointed leader | a chosen leader (as Isaiah 45:1) |

| *'achashdarpenayya'* | satraps | satraps | regional governors (as Esther) |

- **Aramaic.** Daniel 2:4b–7:28 is Aramaic. In the sources, 2:4 is mixed: the
  Hebrew runs to "in Aramaic", and the speech that follows is Aramaic. The
  record at 2:4 is therefore marked `hbo`, and the records from 2:5 to 7:28
  are marked `arc` by `build_skeleton.py`. The meaning decision at 2:4 is the
  one Ezra 4:7–8 has: the words "in Aramaic" mark the change of language, and
  the book stays in Aramaic until 7:28. The first record of 8:1 says the book
  returns to Hebrew. Jeremiah 10:11 is the precedent for an Aramaic record.
- **Names.** Each verse keeps the name its text uses: Daniel or Belteshazzar;
  Hananiah, Mishael and Azariah or Shadrach, Meshach and Abednego. A culture
  decision at 1:7 gives the new names. Nebuchadnezzar, Belshazzar, Darius the
  Mede and Cyrus are spelled as the data gives them. Darius the Mede gets an
  uncertain, footnoted culture decision at 6:1, because no such king is known
  outside Daniel.
- **One like a son of man (7:13).** In the vision this is a description, so it
  is lowercase and takes no `deity_pronoun` span (Isaiah's servant rule). A
  footnoted allusion and theology decision says three things: Jesus takes the
  phrase as his title "the Son of Man" (Matthew 24:30; 26:64); 7:16–27
  interprets the figure alongside "the holy ones of the Most High"; and
  readers differ on how the two relate. The Ancient of Days and the son of man
  of 7:13 are kept distinct at every level.
- **The son of the gods (3:25).** These are Nebuchadnezzar's words, a
  polytheist's. A footnoted theology decision gives that sense and the
  Christian reading of the fourth figure. No level capitalizes the phrase.
- **MENE, MENE, TEKEL, PARSIN (5:25–28).** The Aramaic words are written in
  Latin letters in capitals at every level, with the PERES of 5:28. A
  footnoted idiom decision explains the puns: weights and their related verbs
  "numbered", "weighed" and "divided", and *paras* (Persia). Readable may add
  "which means ..." only where Daniel himself says it.
- **The seventy sevens (9:24–27).** The text gives the number and no unit. A
  footnoted, uncertain theology decision sets out:
  - the usual reading, periods of seven years, drawn from Jeremiah's seventy
    years (9:2);
  - the main ways the "anointed one" of 9:25–26 has been read: the high
    priest Joshua, Cyrus, Onias III, and Christian readings of Jesus.

  The text settles none of them. Nothing in the wording chooses among them,
  and the anointed one takes no `deity_pronoun` span.
- **Chapter 11.** The kings of the north and the south are never named in
  the renderings. Culture decisions give the usual identifications (the
  Ptolemies and the Seleucids, and Antiochus IV from 11:21), marked as
  identifications the text implies but does not state.
- **Resurrection (12:2).** Literal and Balanced keep "those who sleep in the
  dust of the earth will awake". Readable says "many who have died will rise
  to life", with an idiom decision on the image.
- **The Greek additions.** The Prayer of Azariah and the Song of the Three
  Young Men (after 3:23), Susanna and Bel and the Dragon are in the Greek
  versions only. They are not in the source text and are not translated. A
  footnoted text_variant decision at 3:23 says so, and names Susanna and Bel
  and the Dragon.
- **Where Daniel stands.** In the Hebrew Bible, Daniel is among the Writings,
  not the Prophets. A culture decision at 1:1 notes it; the book's place in
  `data/books.json` does not change.
- **New Testament allusions:**
  - 7:13 with Matthew 24:30, 26:64, Mark 13:26, 14:62 and Revelation 1:7, 13;
  - 9:27, 11:31 and 12:11 with Matthew 24:15 and Mark 13:14;
  - 12:1 with Matthew 24:21;
  - 12:2 with John 5:28–29;
  - chapter 7 with Revelation 13.
- **Prose and poetry.** Daniel is prose except for the doxologies and hymns:
  2:20–23; 3:31–33; 4:31–32; 6:27–28; 7:9–10, 13–14. These are set in lines.
- **Versification.** Hebrew 3:31–33 is English 4:1–3, and Hebrew 4:1–34 is
  English 4:4–37. Hebrew 6:1 is English 5:31, and Hebrew 6:2–29 is English
  6:1–28. Footnoted decisions at 3:31, 4:1, 6:1 and 6:2 say so.

## The Twelve

**Headings and dates.** Each book's first verse follows the Jeremiah table:
"The word of the LORD which came to X" (Literal) and "in the days of X". *Massa'* is
"oracle / oracle / message", as in Isaiah, in Nahum 1:1, Habakkuk 1:1,
Zechariah 9:1 and 12:1, and Malachi 1:1. Dates in Haggai and Zechariah keep
the Hebrew form ("in the second year of King Darius, in the sixth month, on
the first day of the month"). A culture decision gives the modern date
(August 520 BC for Haggai 1:1). Readable may say "the sixth month of the
year" but does not convert to a modern calendar.

### Hosea

| Hebrew | Literal | Balanced | Readable |
|---|---|---|---|
| *'eshet zenunim* (1:2) | a wife of prostitution | a promiscuous wife | a wife who will be unfaithful |
| *zanah* | prostitute oneself | prostitute oneself | be unfaithful (as Judges 2:17) |
| *Lo' ruchamah* (1:6) | Lo-ruhamah | No Compassion | No Compassion |
| *Lo' 'ammi* (1:9) | Lo-ammi | Not My People | Not My People |
| *Yizre'e'l* (1:4) | Jezreel | Jezreel | Jezreel |
| *da'at 'elohim* (4:1; 6:6) | knowledge of God | knowledge of God | knowing God |
| *Ephraim* (the northern kingdom) | Ephraim | Ephraim | Ephraim (culture decision at 4:17) |
| *Bet 'aven* (4:15; 5:8; 10:5) | Beth-aven | Beth-aven | Beth-aven (wordplay decision on Bethel) |

- **The children's names.** The names are a message, and 2:3 and 2:25 reverse
  them ("Ammi", "Ruhamah"), so Balanced and Readable translate them. A
  word_sense decision at 1:6 and 1:9 gives the Hebrew form. Literal keeps the
  Hebrew names and gives the meaning in a footnote. This departs, by the
  founder's ruling, from Isaiah, which keeps Maher-shalal-hash-baz at every
  level, because Hosea's text turns on what the names mean.
- **Hosea 1:2.** A footnoted, uncertain meaning decision asks whether Gomer was
  unfaithful before the marriage or became so, which the text does not
  settle.
- **2:18.** "My husband" (*'ishi*) and "my master" (*ba'li*) are a play on the
  name Baal. Every level keeps it, and a footnote explains it.
- **6:6** ("I desire *hesed*, not sacrifice"). *Hesed* is "loyal love" at
  Literal and Balanced and "faithful love" at Readable, as Isaiah. An
  allusion decision notes that Matthew 9:13 and 12:7 quote the Greek
  ("mercy").
- **11:1** ("Out of Egypt I called my son") uses the same wording as
  Matthew 2:15 at each level. The Hebrew and the Greek agree here, and an
  allusion decision notes the quotation.
- **Other New Testament quotations:**
  - 2:1 and 2:25 with Romans 9:25–26 and 1 Peter 2:10;
  - 13:14 with 1 Corinthians 15:55;
  - 10:8 with Luke 23:30.

  Each gets an allusion decision.
- **Versification.** Hebrew 2:1–2 is English 1:10–11, and Hebrew 2:3–25 is
  English 2:1–23. Hebrew 12:1 is English 11:12, and Hebrew 12:2–15 is
  English 12:1–14. Hebrew 14:1 is English 13:16, and Hebrew 14:2–10 is
  English 14:1–9.

### Joel

- **The locusts (1:4; 2:25).** The four names *gazam*, *'arbeh*, *yeleq* and
  *chasil* are "the cutting locust, the swarming locust, the hopping locust,
  the stripping locust" at Literal and Balanced. Readable says "one swarm of
  locusts after another". An uncertain word_sense decision says the names may
  be kinds or stages of locust.
- **3:1–5 (English 2:28–32).** This passage is "on all flesh" at Literal and
  "on all people" at Balanced and Readable. In 3:5, *yimmalet* is "will
  escape" at Literal and "will be saved" at Balanced and Readable, with a
  word_sense decision. An allusion decision notes Acts 2:17–21 (Peter adds
  "in the last days") and Romans 10:13.
- **The valley of Jehoshaphat (4:2, 12).** The name is kept at every level. A
  footnote explains it as "the LORD judges", and 4:14 "the valley of
  decision" refers back to it.
- **Versification.** Hebrew 3:1–5 is English 2:28–32, and Hebrew 4:1–21 is
  English 3:1–21. A footnoted decision at 3:1 and 4:1 says so.

### Amos

| Hebrew | Literal | Balanced | Readable |
|---|---|---|---|
| "for three transgressions of X, and for four" (1:3–2:6) | For three transgressions of X, and for four, I will not turn it back | For three crimes of X, even for four, I will not hold back [the punishment] | The people of X have sinned again and again, so I will not hold back their punishment |
| *pesha'* | transgression | crime / rebellion | sin |
| *mishpat* / *tsedaqah* (5:7, 24; 6:12) | justice / righteousness | justice / righteousness | justice / what is right |
| *ben-navi'* (7:14) | a son of a prophet | a member of a company of prophets | trained as a prophet (as 2 Kings 2:3) |

- **The refrain of chapters 1–2.** It keeps one wording at each level in all
  eight oracles. In "I will not turn it back", the "it" is uncertain, and an
  uncertain decision at 1:3 says so.
- **8:1–2.** "Summer fruit" (*qayits*) and "end" (*qets*) are a pun. Readable
  says the pun in the text, as Jeremiah 1:12 does with the almond tree.
  Literal and Balanced footnote it.
- **New Testament quotations:** 9:11–12 with Acts 15:16–17, where James
  follows the Greek; and 5:25–27 with Acts 7:42–43.

### Obadiah, Jonah, Micah

- **Obadiah.** Verses 1–9 reuse Jeremiah 49:9–16. Edom is also "Esau" and
  "Mount Esau". Every level keeps the names, with a culture decision at
  verse 6.
- **Jonah: the great fish.** *Dag gadol* is "a great fish" at Literal and
  Balanced and "a huge fish" at Readable, never "whale". An allusion decision
  notes that Matthew 12:40 has the Greek "sea creature".
- **Jonah: the size of Nineveh.** In 3:3, *'ir gedolah le'lohim* is "a great
  city to God" at Literal and "an exceedingly great city" at Balanced. An
  idiom decision covers it. "Three days' walk" stays at every level.
- **Jonah: the poem.** Chapter 2 is a psalm and is set in lines. Hebrew 2:1
  is English 1:17, and Hebrew 2:2–11 is English 2:1–10.
- **Jonah 4:2** reuses Exodus 34:6.
- **Micah 5:1 (English 5:2).** The verse is set in lines.
  - *'alfe yehudah* is "the clans of Judah" at Balanced and Readable, with a
    word_sense decision ("thousands").
  - An allusion decision notes that Matthew 2:6 differs ("by no means least
    ... rulers").
  - *motsa'otav miqqedem mime 'olam* is "whose origins are from of old, from
    ancient days" at Literal and Balanced. Readable says "whose family line
    goes back to ancient times".
  - A footnoted, uncertain theology decision gives the reading "from
    eternity". The text allows it but does not require it.
  - The ruler takes no `deity_pronoun` span.
- **Micah 6:8.** *'Ahavat chesed* is "to love loyal love" at Literal, "to
  love loyalty" at Balanced and "to love being kind and loyal" at Readable,
  with a rendering decision on the departure from the settled *hesed*
  wording. "Walk humbly with your God"
  is kept at Literal and Balanced. Readable says "live humbly, obeying your
  God".
- **Micah versification.** Hebrew 4:14 is English 5:1, and Hebrew 5:1–14 is
  English 5:2–15.

### Nahum, Habakkuk, Zephaniah

- **Nahum.** 1:2–3 echoes Exodus 34:6–7. 2:1 reuses Isaiah 52:7. Hebrew 2:1
  is English 1:15, and Hebrew 2:2–14 is English 2:1–13. Chapter 1 is a partial
  acrostic (*aleph* to *kaph*), with the letters named in Latin
  transliteration, as Lamentations. A decision at 1:2 says so and marks it
  uncertain.
- **Habakkuk 2:4** (*wetsaddiq be'emunato yichyeh*):

  | Literal | Balanced | Readable |
  |---|---|---|
  | but the righteous by {{gender:their|his}} faithfulness will live | but the righteous will live by {{gender:their|his}} faithfulness | but the person who does right will live by being faithful |

  - *'Emunah* is "faithfulness", not the Greek key term "faith". A footnoted,
    uncertain word_sense decision gives the range: firmness, trust, loyalty.
  - It also asks whose faithfulness is meant: the Septuagint reads "my
    faithfulness".
  - An allusion decision names Romans 1:17, Galatians 3:11 and Hebrews 10:38,
    where the Greek is "faith".
- **Habakkuk 3.** The chapter follows the psalm titles:
  - "A prayer of Habakkuk the prophet, on shigionoth": *shigionoth* is
    transliterated, as Psalm 7:1 "shiggaion", with "song" at Readable.
  - "Selah" stays at every level.
  - 3:19 reads "For the director: with stringed instruments", as Psalm 4:1.
- **Habakkuk 1:5** gets an allusion decision for Acts 13:41.
- **Zephaniah.** 1:14–18 keeps the Isaiah 13 wording for "the day of the
  LORD". 3:17 ("he will rejoice over you with singing") takes `deity_pronoun`
  spans throughout.

### Haggai, Zechariah, Malachi

- **Names.** The high priest is "Joshua son of Jehozadak" in Haggai and
  Zechariah, and "Jeshua" in Ezra. Each book keeps its own form, with a
  culture decision at Haggai 1:1 linking the two. Zerubbabel is "son of
  Shealtiel", as in Ezra.
- **Zechariah's night visions (1:7–6:8):**
  - *hammal'akh haddover bi* is "the angel who talked with me" at every
    level.
  - *hassatan* (3:1–2) follows Job: "the satan" at Literal and "the Accuser"
    at Balanced and Readable.
  - The horns, the measuring line, the lampstand, the flying scroll and the
    woman in the basket keep their images at every level. Each vision gets a
    meaning decision, and the angel's explanation is translated, not
    expanded.
- **Zechariah 9:9.** "Daughter Zion" and "Daughter Jerusalem" follow Isaiah.
  *'ani werokhev 'al-chamor* is "humble and riding on a donkey, on a colt, the
  foal of a donkey" at every level. An allusion decision notes Matthew 21:5
  and John 12:15, and Matthew's two animals.
- **Zechariah 11:12–13.** The "thirty [pieces of] silver" and "the potter" get
  an allusion decision. Matthew 27:9 names Jeremiah; a footnote gives the
  usual explanation.
- **Zechariah 12:10.** "They will look on me, the one they pierced". An
  uncertain, footnoted text_variant decision covers "on me" and "on him", and
  an allusion decision notes John 19:37 and Revelation 1:7.
- **Zechariah 13:7** gets an allusion decision for Matthew 26:31 and Mark
  14:27.
- **Zechariah versification.** Hebrew 2:1–4 is English 1:18–21, and Hebrew
  2:5–17 is English 2:1–13.
- **Malachi's disputations.** Each one follows the pattern: God's statement,
  then "But you say, '...?'", then the answer. The people's questions take
  double quotes. God's words take none, as in Isaiah.
- **Malachi 1:2–3.** "I loved Jacob, but Esau I hated" is kept at Literal and
  Balanced. Readable says "I chose to love Jacob, but I rejected Esau", as
  Romans 9:13. An idiom decision explains that "love" and "hate" here mean
  choosing and not choosing.
- **Malachi 2:16.** The Hebrew is hard. Literal is "For [he] hates divorce,
  says the LORD", and the other levels follow the reading the drafter judges
  best. An uncertain, footnoted text_variant and grammar decision gives the
  main reading ("the one who hates and divorces covers his garment with
  violence").
- **Malachi 3:1.** "My messenger" (*mal'akhi*, also the book's name) and "the
  messenger of the covenant" are both lowercase. An allusion decision notes
  Mark 1:2 and Matthew 11:10, which reuse its Greek.
- **Malachi 3:23–24 (English 4:5–6).** "Elijah the prophet" is kept at every
  level. 3:24 ("turn the hearts of fathers to children") gets an allusion
  decision for Luke 1:17 and Matthew 17:11. A footnoted decision at 3:19 and
  3:23 gives the English numbering (Hebrew 3:19–24 is English 4:1–6).

## Settled while drafting Hosea to Jonah

These conventions came up while the first five books were drafted. They are
AI-draft choices, recorded here so the later books follow them; the founder
may change any of them.

- "The LORD God of hosts" (no comma, as in the Psalms): "the LORD God of
  hosts" at Literal, "the LORD God of Armies" at Balanced and Readable.
  "The God of hosts" alone is "the God of hosts" / "the God of Armies";
  *Adonai YHWH hatseva'ot* (Amos 9:5) is "the Lord GOD of hosts" / "of
  Armies".
- *'Adon* for God (Hosea 12:15) is "Lord".
- *'Oy* aimed at others (Hosea 7:13) follows Isaiah's *hoy*: "Woe to them"
  / "Woe to them" / "How terrible it will be for them". The funeral cry *ho
  ho* (Amos 5:16) follows Jeremiah 22:18: "Alas" / "Alas" / "How sad".
- *Sar* is "officials" at Literal and Balanced and "leaders" at Readable.
- *Shofar*: on its own, "trumpet" / "ram's horn" / "trumpet" (Isaiah,
  Jeremiah 4:5); beside a metal trumpet (Hosea 5:8), "ram's horn" at every
  level.
- The verb *pasha'* is "transgress" / "rebel" / "sin".
- *'Anakh* (Amos 7:7–8) is "plumb line" at every level, with an uncertain
  note giving "tin".
- *Zanah* of literal prostitution (Amos 7:17; Hosea 4:13–14 at Readable)
  says so plainly ("be a prostitute", "act like prostitutes"); the Hosea
  table's "be unfaithful" is for Israel's unfaithfulness to God.
- The Amos refrain's Balanced line is "I will not hold back the punishment",
  with no brackets: supplied words are bracketed only at Literal.
- Lines a book repeats keep the wording of their first use at each level:
  Hosea 6:10 and 5:3, 7:10 and 5:5, 8:13 and 9:9; Joel 4:16 and Amos 1:2;
  Amos 8:6 and 2:6 (Literal). Obadiah 1–9 reuses Jeremiah 49; Jonah's psalm
  reuses the published Psalms where it quotes them.
- Fixed formulas may share a run of twelve or more words with an older
  Bible (Hosea 1:1, Amos 1:1, the Amos 1–2 refrain at Literal, Amos 3:13,
  Jonah 1:1). Every other such run is reworded.
- Headings stand only where the Hebrew starts a paragraph (pe or samekh).
- **Open for the founder:** quotations of Exodus 34:6 differ on *hesed*.
  Joel 2:13 uses the prophets' "loyal love"; Jonah 4:2 reuses the published
  Exodus 34:6 ("steadfast love" at Literal). One wording should be chosen
  for Joel, Jonah, Nahum 1:3 and Exodus 34:6 itself.

## The founder's rulings (2026-10-15)

1. **Hosea's children.** Balanced and Readable translate the names ("No
   Compassion", "Not My People"); Literal keeps the Hebrew names.
2. **"Son of man".** Kept at every level in Ezekiel and in Daniel 8:17,
   Readable included.
3. ***Hesed* in Micah 6:8.** "to love loyal love" / "to love loyalty" / "to
   love being kind and loyal", with a decision.
4. **The bath and the ephah.** One figure, about 6 gallons, for both in
   Ezekiel 45–46. The published books keep "about 20 quarts" for the ephah
   until a separate cleanup decides whether to change them.
5. **Number words in unit spans.** New spans write the alternative in words
   up to ninety-nine. The published books' mixed forms are left for a
   separate cleanup.
