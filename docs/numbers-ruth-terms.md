# Numbers to Ruth: recurring terms and book-wide choices

**Status: proposed, awaiting the founder's approval.** Numbers, Deuteronomy,
Joshua, Judges and Ruth were drafted chapter by chapter in parallel from one
brief, and these are the choices that keep them consistent with each other and
with Exodus and Leviticus. If a choice changes, every chapter drafted under it
is revised. Read with `docs/english-style.md`, which wins where the two differ.

## Units and numbers

Literal keeps the Hebrew unit with no setting span. Balanced and Readable give
the modern measure with the original as the alternative:
`{{units:about 18 inches|a cubit}}`.

| Unit | Modern default |
|---|---|
| cubit | about 18 inches (2 cubits: about 3 feet; 1,000 cubits: about 1,500 feet; 2,000: about 3,000 feet) |
| shekel | about 2/5 ounce (130 shekels: about 3 1/4 pounds; 70: about 1 3/4 pounds; 10: about 4 ounces) |
| gerah | a twentieth of a shekel |
| ephah | about 20 quarts (a tenth: about 2 quarts) |
| hin | about a gallon (a half: about 2 quarts; a third: about 1 1/4 quarts; a quarter: about a quart) |
| homer | about 6 bushels |
| talent | about 75 pounds |

"The shekel of the sanctuary" is "by the sanctuary shekel" at Literal; Balanced
and Readable fold it into the span (`{{units:about 3 1/4 pounds by the sanctuary standard|130 shekels by the sanctuary shekel}}`).
Numbers are in words up to ninety-nine and numerals above: 46,500.

## Numbers

- *'elef* in the census lists (chapters 1, 2, 26) is "thousand" at every
  level. Chapter 1's first census record has an uncertain, footnoted meaning
  decision on the size of the totals (603,550 men): the word can also name a
  military or clan unit, which would give much smaller numbers. The other
  chapters refer back to it rather than repeat it.
- *Tsava'* in "everyone going out to war" is military service: "everyone who
  can go out to war" (Literal: "every one going out [to the] army").
- *Nazir*: "Nazirite" at every level; a meaning decision at 6:2 says it means
  "one set apart".
- The priestly blessing (6:24–26), the ark sayings (10:35–36), the songs of
  21:14–15, 17–18, 27–30 and Balaam's oracles (23:7–10, 18–24; 24:3–9,
  15–24) are poetry: set them in lines (`\q`) as `docs/english-style.md`
  describes, at Literal and Balanced at least.
- *Sheol* (16:30, 33): Literal and Balanced "Sheol", Readable "the grave",
  with a meaning decision.
- *Shaddai*: "the Almighty"; Readable "God All-Powerful". *'Elyon*: "the
  Most High".
- Cities of refuge (chapter 35): "cities of refuge" at Literal and Balanced,
  "towns of safety" at Readable; *go'el haddam* "the avenger of blood",
  Readable "the relative who avenges the death".
- **Versification.** Hebrew 17:1–15 is English 16:36–50 and Hebrew 17:16–28 is
  English 17:1–13; Hebrew 25:19 is the start of English 26:1; Hebrew 30:1 is
  English 29:40 and Hebrew 30:2–17 is English 30:1–16 (`data/versification.json`).
  A footnoted decision at 17:1, 17:16, 25:19, 30:1 and 30:2 says so, and the
  book is cited by the Hebrew numbers.

## Deuteronomy

- *Torah* in "this law", "the book of this law" is "law" at Literal and
  Balanced and "teaching" or "laws" at Readable, with a word_sense decision at
  1:5 (instruction, the whole of what Moses teaches, not only rules).
- *Shema'* (6:4): "Hear, Israel:" (no "O"). The confession has footnoted
  meaning decisions on its two readings ("the LORD our God, the LORD is one"
  and "the LORD is our God, the LORD alone"); Literal keeps the bare Hebrew
  order with brackets.
- "Beyond the Jordan" (1:1, 1:5 and elsewhere) is "across the Jordan"; a
  decision says the viewpoint is the land west of it.
- *Yarash*: "take possession of" (the land) and "dispossess" or "drive out"
  (nations).
- Asherah: "Asherah poles" at Literal and Balanced, "poles used to worship
  the goddess Asherah" at Readable, as Exodus 34:13.
- The Song of Moses (32:1–43) and the Blessing of Moses (33:2–29) are poetry,
  set in lines.
- *Yeshurun* (32:15; 33:5, 26): "Jeshurun" at Literal and Balanced, "Israel"
  at Readable with a decision ("the upright one", a name for Israel).
- **Versification.** Hebrew 13:1 is English 12:32 and Hebrew 13:2–19 is
  English 13:1–18; Hebrew 23:1 is English 22:30 and Hebrew 23:2–26 is English
  23:1–25; Hebrew 28:69 is English 29:1 and Hebrew 29:1–28 is English
  29:2–29. A footnoted decision at the first verse of each shift says so.

## Joshua

- *Cherem* as above; the story of Achan (chapter 7) keeps "devoted things"
  at every level.
- "Joshua son of Nun"; "the servant of Moses" (1:1) follows the *'eved* rule.
- The sun and moon saying (10:12–13) is poetry, with a decision on the
  Book of Jashar.
- The boundary and town lists (chapters 13–21) keep every name as the data
  spells it in English; Readable may add "the town of" where a bare name
  would confuse.

## Judges

- *Shofet* and the verb *shafat*: "judge" / "judged Israel" at Literal and
  Balanced, "led Israel" at Readable, with a word_sense decision at 2:16 (a
  leader raised to rescue Israel and govern it, more than a court judge).
- "Did what was evil in the eyes of the LORD": Literal "did the evil in [the]
  eyes of", Balanced "did what was evil in the sight of", Readable "did what
  the LORD said was evil".
- "The land was quiet ... years": Literal "the land was quiet", Balanced and
  Readable "the land had peace".
- *Mal'akh YHWH*: "the angel of the LORD" at every level, with a meaning
  decision on his identity at 2:1, 6:11 and 13:3.
- "The Spirit of the LORD came on ..." keeps "Spirit" with a capital S.
- "In those days there was no king in Israel; each did what was right in his
  own eyes" (17:6; 21:25): Balanced "everyone did what they thought was right".
- *Pilegesh*: "concubine" at Literal and Balanced; Readable "concubine (a
  secondary wife)" the first time in a chapter.
- The Song of Deborah (chapter 5) is poetry, set in lines; so are Jotham's
  fable (9:8–15) and Samson's riddles and sayings (14:14, 18; 15:16).

## Ruth

- *Go'el*: Literal "redeemer", Balanced and Readable "family redeemer", with a
  culture decision at 2:20 (the relative bound to buy back family land and
  care for the family line, Leviticus 25:25; Deuteronomy 25:5–10).
- *Chesed*: "loyal love" at Literal and Balanced; Readable "kindness" or
  "faithful love" where it reads better, with a decision at 1:8.
- *Shaddai* (1:20–21): "the Almighty"; Readable "God All-Powerful".
- "Spread your wing/corner over" (3:9) keeps the picture at Literal and
  Balanced with an idiom decision linking 2:12.
