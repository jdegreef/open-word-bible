# Chinese terms: Genesis to Revelation

**Status: proposed, awaiting native Chinese reviewers.** Nothing in Chinese
is drafted yet; every form below is a first proposal, written with the
style guide before drafting so that books drafted in parallel agree. Every
Chinese record will be `ai_draft`. If a choice here changes, every passage
drafted under it is revised.

Reviewers: start with
[`docs/review-notes/zh-review-checklist.md`](review-notes/zh-review-checklist.md).

## How this glossary relates to the style guide

`docs/chinese-style.md` is the brief for drafting Chinese (Simplified
characters, Mainland usage) and holds in full: the three levels (直译,
均衡, 易读), register, address, the divine name and the setting spans,
punctuation, numbers, the New Testament's words (section 7) and the
formulas (section 12). This glossary is the single reference for the words,
formulas and names the whole Bible needs beyond those. `docs/hindi-terms.md`,
`docs/russian-terms.md` and `docs/indonesian-terms.md` are its counterparts
in other languages; each book's own terms note (`docs/isaiah-terms.md` and
the others) still settles the meaning, and this file settles only the
Chinese wording.

Where a form is marked *alt.*, that is the main alternative a reviewer
should weigh. Where this translation's New Testament or an earlier book
already has a form, that form wins. Decision text stays in English, quoting
the Chinese words.

Below, *dn* is `{{divine_name:上主|雅威}}` and *gt* is `{{god_term:神|上帝}}`
(style guide section 4). Levels are L (直译), B (均衡) and R (易读).

## Drafting

- Translate from the Hebrew (and the Aramaic in Daniel 2:4-7:28, Ezra
  4:8-6:18 and 7:12-26, and Jeremiah 10:11) and the Greek, using the literal
  gloss, the English and the meaning decisions to see how a sentence was
  read. Never from a reference Bible. **The Chinese Union Version (和合本)
  is for comparison only**, though its text is in the public domain in many
  places; the copyrighted versions (和合本修订版, 新译本, 当代译本, 现代中文译本,
  中文标准译本, 思高本 and the others) likewise. The familiar passages pull
  hardest toward the CUV: Genesis 1-3, Exodus 20, Deuteronomy 6, Numbers
  6:24-26; Psalms 1, 23, 51, 91, 103, 121, 139; Proverbs 3:5-6, 31;
  Ecclesiastes 1-3 and 12; Isaiah 9, 40, 53; Jeremiah 31; Micah 5-6; the
  Sermon on the Mount, John 1 and 3, Romans 8, 1 Corinthians 13. Draft from
  the gloss and the English levels and rewrite any sentence that matches a
  well-known version for more than a short common phrase.
- The vocabulary in this file is the church's shared vocabulary (恩典, 燔祭,
  会幕, 约柜, 舍客勒), and the names are the conventional forms; using them is
  not copying wording. A clause is wording.
- Keep every `\v N ` marker where the English has it. Lines (`\q `): see
  [Poetry conventions](#poetry-conventions).
- Quotation marks “ ” outside and ‘ ’ inside, written curly in the data.
  Each level opens and closes the same quotations as the English level.
- Settings mirror the English spans where Chinese has the same choice:
  `divine_name`, `deity_pronoun` (他 / 祂, 你 / 祢), `units`, `christos`, and
  `gender` where Chinese really has two wordings; and the Chinese-only
  `god_term` for every word naming the one God.
- Narrative: plain past without a 了 on every verb; 了 where the action's
  completion matters, 就 and 于是 for sequence.
- Laws and instructions (Exodus 20-40, Leviticus, much of Numbers,
  Deuteronomy's speeches): 你要…… / 不可…… / 他要把……带来 at L and B;
  你必须…… / 不要…… / 他应当…… at R.
- Rendering decisions: zero to two per sentence, only where Chinese had to
  choose. A book-wide choice is recorded once, in the first passage file of
  each book; later chapters follow it.
- Measures: metric by default where the English converts, in Arabic digits
  (约45厘米), the original unit as the alternative in Chinese numerals;
  Literal keeps the original unit where the English Literal does. Where the
  English has no `units` span, neither does the Chinese. See
  [Land, life and measures](#land-life-and-measures).

## Address and pronouns

The style guide section 5 holds in full; in brief:

- **God in the third person**: 他 in a `deity_pronoun` span,
  `{{deity_pronoun:他|祂}}`, wherever the English spans "he / him / his".
- **People speaking or praying to God**: 你 in a span, `{{deity_pronoun:你|祢}}`,
  at every level (David, the psalmists, Job, Moses, the prophets). 您 is not
  used for God.
- **God speaking**: 我, no span; to people 你 / 你们, one person or many;
  also to the Servant in Isaiah 42-53.
- **To a king**: 王啊 (L); 陛下 and 您 (B, R). "My lord the king": 我主我王
  (L, B), 陛下 (R). "Let the king live": 愿王万岁 (L, B), 祝陛下万岁 (R).
- **To a ruler or master**: 你 at L; 您 at B and R. Moses and Aaron say 您
  to Pharaoh at B and R, and Pharaoh says 你们 to them. Joseph's brothers say
  您 to Joseph in Egypt (B, R) until he makes himself known (Genesis 45:4),
  where the change of address is noted.
- **Family and equals**: 你. Isaac to Abraham (Genesis 22:7) says 你 at L,
  您 at B and R; Esau and Jacob say 你. Nathan says 你 to David ("你就是那个人！",
  2 Samuel 12:7, at every level: a prophet's rebuke drops the courtesy).
- **"Your servant" for "I"** (*ʿavdeka*): 仆人 / 你的仆人 at L and where the
  English Balanced keeps it; 我 at R.
- **The psalmist to people and to his enemies**: 你们 / 你. The lovers in the
  Song of Songs: 你.
- **Lines the New Testament quotes keep its forms of address** (Psalm 2:7 in
  Hebrews 1:5; Psalm 45:6-7 in Hebrews 1:8-9).

### Kings and Chronicles: the regnal formulas

One wording per level through Kings and Chronicles. B smooths; R may split
the sentence.

- **Accession.** "In the Nth year of X king of Judah, Y son of Z became king
  over Israel in Samaria, and he reigned N years." L: 犹大王X第N年，Z的儿子Y
  在撒玛利亚登基，作以色列的王，共作王N年。 R: X作犹大王的第N年，Z的儿子Y
  在撒玛利亚当了以色列的王，统治了N年。
- **Age and mother (Judah).** 他登基的时候N岁，在耶路撒冷作王N年。他的母亲
  名叫X。

  在*dn*眼中为恶 / 为正的事 (L); 他做了*dn*看为恶 / 看为正的事 (B); 他做了

- **Death.** "He slept with his fathers and was buried": 他与他的
  `{{gender:祖先|列祖}}`同睡，被埋葬在…… (L, B), where the English has a span;
  他去世了，葬在…… (R).
- **Genealogies (1 Chronicles 1-9)** keep one list formula per level: "the
  sons of X: A, B and C" X的儿子是A、B和C (the enumeration comma 、 between
  names, 和 before the last); "X fathered Y" X生Y (L, as Matthew 1), X是Y的

## Poetry conventions

- **Lines follow the English record.** Where an English level has `\q`
  breaks, that Chinese level has the same lines, broken at Chinese's own
  phrase boundaries; where the English level is prose, so is the Chinese.
  A space on both sides of each marker.
- **Parallel lines** are not forced into equal character counts or into
  four-character phrases: Chinese verse habits (对仗, rhyme) are not imposed on
  Hebrew poetry.
- **Acrostics** (Lamentations 1-4, Psalms 9-10, 25, 34, 37, 111, 112, 119,
  145, Proverbs 31:10-31): follow the English; no letter headings unless it
  has them.
- **Personified women.** Wisdom and Folly are women in the Hebrew: 她 and
  她们; Zion and Jerusalem pictured as a woman take 她, as Lamentations.

## Names

**Policy** (style guide section 6). The forms in common use in the
Protestant Chinese Bibles for every well-known name; the modern standard
Chinese form for places that still exist where it is far better known
(大马士革, 塞浦路斯, 克里特, 马耳他); one person, one form, in every book;
names the New Testament also has keep the New Testament's form. New
transliterations by the style guide's rules, added to
[Added while drafting](#added-while-drafting).

- **Two names for one person.** Keep each name where the source has it:
  Abram 亚伯兰 / Abraham 亚伯拉罕; Sarai 撒莱 / Sarah 撒拉; Jacob 雅各 / Israel
  以色列; Uzziah 乌西雅 / Azariah 亚撒利雅 (Kings); Nebuchadnezzar 尼布甲尼撒 /
  Nebuchadrezzar 尼布甲尼撒 (one Chinese form; a decision notes the Hebrew
  spelling where it differs); Saul 扫罗 / Paul 保罗; Simon 西门 / Peter 彼得 /
  Cephas 矶法.
- **Same name, two people.** Chinese Bibles distinguish the apostle Philip
  腓力 from Philip the evangelist 腓利 (Acts 6:5; 8; 21:8); the distinction is
  kept, as readers know it. Otherwise one name, one form (James and Jacob
  are both 雅各).
- Beersheba 别是巴 is kept apart from Bathsheba 拔示巴.

## The New Testament quotes the Old

**Policy.** Where the New Testament quotes a line and the Hebrew says the
same, use the New Testament's Chinese wording at the matching level. Where
the New Testament follows the Greek Old Testament and differs, translate the
Hebrew and add a decision naming the New Testament form (Psalm 40:6 "a
body"; Isaiah 7:14 "virgin"; Amos 9:11-12 in Acts 15; and the others listed
in `docs/hindi-terms.md`, "The New Testament quotes the Old"). Quoted lines
keep the New Testament's forms of address.

- **Daniel.** Aramaic 2:4-7:28. "The Ancient of Days" above; "the
  abomination of desolation" as Matthew 24:15 where quoted.
- **The Twelve.** Hosea's hesed is Israel's loyalty (above). Jonah 2 and
  Habakkuk 3 are poetry. Malachi 3:1 and 4:5-6 (Hebrew 3:23-24) keep the

- **New Testament.** `docs/chinese-style.md` sections 5-8 and 12, and the
  book terms notes. The Gospels' Aramaic words (Mark 5:41; 7:34; 15:34)
  are transliterated by the style guide's rules from the Greek, as the
  English level gives them, with the translation the Greek supplies.
