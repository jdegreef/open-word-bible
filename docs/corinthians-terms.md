# 1 and 2 Corinthians: recurring terms and book-wide choices

**Status: proposed, awaiting the founder's approval, except where a choice
says it is settled.** These are the choices
the drafts so far (1 Corinthians 1–12, all of 2 Corinthians) settled on, so that
later chapters stay consistent with them. If a choice changes, every passage
drafted under it is revised. Read with `docs/english-style.md`; key terms are
enforced through `data/key_terms.json`.

## Choices made in context

**Corinthian slogans.** Where Paul quotes the Corinthians' own sayings
(1 Cor 6:12–13; 7:1; 8:1, 4), Literal and Balanced put them in quotation
marks and Readable adds "you say" or "some of you say", each with a
punctuation decision, footnoted, and uncertain where the extent of the quote
is debated.

**1 Corinthians 6:9–10.** The two words for male sexual partners stay two
items at every level, describe acts and not orientation, and never become
"homosexuals", "effeminate" or "sodomites"; the meaning decisions give the
evidence and the debated alternatives, footnoted.

**The Holy Spirit** takes the deity_pronoun setting like God and Christ, and is never "it" (2 Cor 1:22; 1 Cor 12:11).

**Names.** "Cephas" at Literal and Balanced, "Peter" at Readable (1 Cor 1:12;
3:22). "Silvanus", not "Silas" (2 Cor 1:19).

**The party slogans** (1 Cor 1:12; 3:4): "I am of Paul" (Literal), "I am for
Paul" (Balanced), "I am on Paul's side" (Readable), in the same words each
time they come back.

**Boasting (*kauchaomai*, *kauchēma*, *kauchēsis*). Settled by the founder,
11 October 2026.** In English "boast" sounds bad, but Paul also uses the word
for good pride (2 Cor 1:12–14), so:
- Literal always says "boast/boasting", so the word can be traced.
- Balanced says "boast" where Paul's argument turns on the word itself
  (1 Cor 1:29–31; 2 Cor 10–12, where his ironic boasting carries the
  argument), and "take pride / be proud / reason to be proud" elsewhere.
- Readable says "be proud", or "brag" for pride that takes credit for a gift
  (1 Cor 3:21; 4:7), except where Balanced keeps "boast".
- Each departure from "boast" gets an English rendering decision.
- In 2 Corinthians 10–12 the drafts keep "boast" at all three levels.

**The earlier letter and the painful visit** (2 Cor 2:1–9). "I wrote" is read
as an earlier, tearful letter, not 1 Corinthians (uncertain, footnoted);
7:8–12 should match. 2:1 is read as an earlier painful visit (12:14; 13:1–2).

***Charis* as "thanks"** (2 Cor 2:14; 8:16; 9:15): a word_sense meaning
decision and an English rendering decision each time, since *charis* is a key
term.

**Paul's "we"** in 2 Corinthians is mostly Paul himself, perhaps with his
companions (meaning decision at 1:8, uncertain).

**The offender** (2 Cor 2:5–11): "this man", with no gender span; the Greek is
masculine and means one person.

**Old Testament quotations**: *kyrios* is "Lord", with a decision giving the
divine name behind it (1 Cor 1:31; 2:16; 3:20; 2 Cor 3:16–18), as John 1:23
does.
