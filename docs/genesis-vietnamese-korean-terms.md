# Genesis in Vietnamese and Korean

**Status: proposed, awaiting native Vietnamese and Korean reviewers.** These
are the choices used to draft Genesis in Vietnamese and Korean, the first
Old Testament narrative in either language, so that chapter blocks drafted
in parallel read as one translation. `docs/vietnamese-style.md` and
`docs/korean-style.md` still hold (section 7a for the Old Testament), and so
do the Vietnamese and Korean columns of
`docs/psalms-proverbs-five-languages-terms.md` where Genesis shares a word
with Psalms or Proverbs. `docs/pentateuch-fr-es-sw-terms.md` shows how
French, Spanish and Swahili handled the same book; its measures apply here
too. If a choice here changes, every passage drafted under it is revised.

Decision text stays in English, quoting the Vietnamese or Korean words.

## Both languages

- Translate from the Hebrew, using the literal gloss, the English and the
  meaning decisions to see how a sentence was read. Never from a reference
  Bible, and never reproduce a copyrighted Bible's wording (Vietnamese:
  Bản Truyền Thống Hiệu Đính, Bản Dịch Mới, Bản Phổ Thông, Bản Hiện Đại,
  Công Giáo; Korean: 개역개정, 새번역, 공동번역, 현대인의 성경, 쉬운성경,
  우리말성경). Genesis 1–3, 12, 22 and 50 pull hardest: draft from the gloss
  and rewrite any line that matches a well-known version for more than a
  short common phrase. The public-domain Vietnamese 1925 Bible and Korean
  개역한글 wording may echo in a few short phrases but is never the basis.
- **Names** keep the forms the published New Testament, Psalms and Proverbs
  already use (table below). A name not in the table follows the usual
  Protestant form in the language and stays the same through the book: keep
  a list as you draft.
- **Narrative.** Vietnamese tells the story in plain past narration, with
  *đã* only where the sequence needs it; Korean narration is the plain
  written style (해라체, -었다) with the honorific -시- for God at every level.
- **Speech** takes double quotation marks, as in the Gospels. Long speeches
  run on across verses without closing the quotation at each verse; a
  verse that is wholly inside a speech has no opening mark.
- **Address forms between people** are a rendering choice in both languages
  and are recorded once per pair, at their first exchange, in each block:
  husband and wife, parent and child, brothers, master and servant, a host
  and a stranger, Joseph and his brothers before and after he makes himself
  known. Follow the relationship the Hebrew shows, not the reader's culture
  today. Rules for the main pairs are below.
- **God speaking** uses Vietnamese *Ta* (with `{{deity_pronoun:ta|Ta}}`
  mid-sentence) and calls a person *ngươi* (L) or *con* (B, R) as the
  relationship allows; Korean God speaks in 해라체 with 나 and 너. People
  speak to God in prayer forms: Vietnamese *Ngài* or *Chúa*, *con* for
  themselves; Korean 합쇼체 with 저/제 and 주님.
- **Lines** follow the English record: the English Genesis is not yet set
  in lines (no `\q`, even in the poems of 2:23, 4:23–24 and 49), so the
  Vietnamese and Korean are prose too.
- Rendering decisions: zero to two per sentence, only where the language had
  to choose. A book-wide choice is recorded once in each block of chapters
  (its first use); later uses just follow it.

**Names that are explained** (Eve, Cain, Isaac, Jacob, Israel, the twelve
sons, Peniel, Ephraim, Manasseh) keep the name and explain the wordplay as
the English level does: Literal keeps the name with the Hebrew meaning in
brackets where the English does, Balanced and Readable say what the name
sounds like, and the English meaning decision is footnoted in the language.

## Address between people

**Vietnamese**
- Husband to wife: *em* at B and R, *ngươi* never; wife to husband: *anh*
  (B, R); Literal may keep *ông* / *bà* where the English Literal keeps the
  formality (Abraham and Sarah's "my lord" in 18:12).
- Parent to child: *cha*/*mẹ* and *con*; child to parent: *cha*/*mẹ* and
  *con*.
- Brothers: the older says *anh* to himself and *em* to the younger;
  Joseph's brothers, speaking to the Egyptian governor, say *ngài* and call
  themselves *chúng tôi* / *đầy tớ ngài*; Joseph to them, unknown, says
  *các ngươi* and *ta*; after 45:1, *anh em* and *tôi*.
- Master and servant: master *ta* / *ngươi*, servant *tôi* / *ông* (B, R).
- Strangers and hosts (18–19; 23; 24): *ông*, *các ông*, *tôi*.

**Korean**
- God and people, and people praying, as above.
- Husband and wife: 해요체 with 당신 between them at B and R (Literal may
  keep the plain style the English Literal suggests); Sarah's "my lord" in
  18:12 is 내 주인.
- Parent to child: 해라체 with 너; child to parent: 합쇼체 or 해요체 with
  아버지/어머니 and 저.
- Brothers: the older speaks 해라체 to the younger; the younger 해요체 to
  the older. Joseph's brothers to the governor: 합쇼체 with 나리 and 저희 /
  당신의 종들; Joseph to them, unknown, 해라체 with 너희; after 45:1, 해요체
  or 해라체 as an older or younger brother.
- Master and servant: master 해라체; servant 합쇼체 with 주인님 and 저.
- Strangers and hosts: 합쇼체.

## Measures

As `docs/pentateuch-fr-es-sw-terms.md`: metric by default where the English
converts, the original unit as the alternative; Literal keeps the original
unit, as the English does. Noah's ark (6:15): cubit 45 cm; shekel 11.5 g
(23:15–16; 24:22; 37:28). Vietnamese writes decimals with a comma; Korean
with a point. Days, years and ages are not units.

## Settled while drafting

AI-draft choices recorded so later work follows them; reviewers may change
any of them.

## Before review

Only rendering decisions were added; the meaning decisions are the English
records' own. Reviewers should look first at the address forms between
people, the explained names, and the decisions marked uncertain.
