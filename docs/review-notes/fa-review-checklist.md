# Persian review checklist

The starting point for native Persian reviewers. **This is an outline
written before drafting began**: it lists the book-wide choices the drafts
will follow, in the order a reviewer should settle them, so that the most
consequential ones can be settled before many books are drafted, and review
of the text can start as soon as it is. The recurring uncertain decisions,
the inconsistencies and the passage list are filled in from `content/` once
books are drafted.

Most of the Persian wording will follow a few dozen book-wide choices: the
divine name, address, the verbs for God's speech, the key terms, the
formulas, the name forms. A reviewer who settles those first settles the
wording of thousands of verses at once, so start with section 1.

How to use it:

- **Work down section 1 first.** Each item decides wording in many verses.
  Tick it when you agree, or write the change you want beside it.
- **Then section 2**, spelling and typography, which a reviewer can check on
  any page.
- **Then sections 3 and 4**: places where the drafts disagree with each
  other, and the famous passages to check against the Old Persian Version
  and Muzhdeh for originality.
- The full list of Persian decisions marked uncertain, with links, will be
  `docs/review-notes/fa-uncertain.md`, generated by
  `python3 scripts/review_checklist.py fa --write` (do not edit it by hand).
- **Changing a choice** means editing the doc that records it
  (`docs/persian-style.md` or `docs/persian-terms.md`) and then revising
  every passage drafted under it. A choice is not changed by editing one
  verse.
- Records stay `ai_draft` until a named human reviewer approves them.
  Nothing here is marked reviewed by Claude.

## 1. Book-wide choices

- [ ] **The divine name**: `{{divine_name:خداوند|یهوه}}`, خداوند by default
  (style guide section 4).
- [ ] ***ʾAdonai* YHWH**: `خداوند {{divine_name:خدا|یهوه}}` ("the Lord God").
- [ ] **No christos span**: مسیح only, as Hindi has मसीह (style guide
  section 9).
- [ ] **No deity_pronoun span**: Persian has no capitals and one او.
- [ ] **Address**: تو to God at every level; تو or شما to Jesus by speaker
  (section 5); شما to kings and masters at B and R.
- [ ] **Verbs**: فرمود for God's speech at L and B; گفت for Jesus.
- [ ] **Brothers**: `{{gender:برادران و خواهران|برادران}}`.
- [ ] **Level names**: تحت‌اللفظی, متعادل, روان.
- [ ] **Key terms** (style guide section 8): فیض, پارسایی, ایمان, توبه,
  جلال, ملکوت خدا / پادشاهی خدا, انجیل / خبر خوش, روح‌القدس, حیات جاودان,
  محبت (for *agapē*), *hesed* in three forms.
- [ ] **Formulas** (style guide section 11): چنین می‌گوید / می‌فرماید,
  این فرمودهٔ خداوند است, کلام خداوند بر ... نازل شد, براستی به شما می‌گویم.
- [ ] **Names** (glossary): the Bible forms (عیسی, یحیی, پطرس, پولس), the
  Persian kings (کوروش, داریوش, خشایارشا, اردشیر), Philistines, Lazarus.
- [ ] **Register**: written standard Persian at every level; Readable plain
  but not colloquial. Is Readable understandable to Afghan readers?
- [ ] **Numerals**: words in the text, Persian digits in modern measures;
  should the site show verse numbers in Persian digits?

## 2. Spelling and typography

- [ ] ی and ک, never ي and ك; Persian digits ۰-۹, never ٠-٩.
- [ ] The half-space in می‌, نمی‌, ‌ها, ‌ام, ‌ای and compounds.
- [ ] Ezafe written only as هٔ after silent ه; no vowel signs.
- [ ] Punctuation: ، ؛ ؟, « » outside and “ ” inside.
- [ ] No old forms at B and R: همی-, بگفت, ایشان for one person.

## 3. Inconsistencies between books

Filled in once books are drafted (`scripts/name_spellings.py`,
`scripts/settings_parity.py`).

## 4. Passages to read closely

The familiar passages, against the Old Persian Version and Muzhdeh for
originality: Genesis 1-3, Exodus 20, Numbers 6:24-26, Deuteronomy 6:4-9,
Psalms 1, 23, 51, 91, 121, Isaiah 9:1-7, 40, 52:13-53:12, Matthew 5-7,
Luke 2, John 1:1-18, 3:16, Romans 8, 1 Corinthians 13. And the passages
where Persia itself appears: Isaiah 44:28-45:7 (Cyrus), Ezra 1, Esther,
Daniel 5-6, Matthew 2:1-12 (the magi, مُغ‌ها).
