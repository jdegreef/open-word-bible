# Nehemiah, Esther, Job, Ecclesiastes and Song of Songs in French, Spanish, Swahili, Vietnamese and Korean

**Status: proposed, awaiting native reviewers.** These are the choices used to
draft Nehemiah, Esther, Job, Ecclesiastes and Song of Songs in five languages
at once, so that chapter blocks drafted in parallel read as one translation in
each language. They continue the earlier five-language notes, which hold here
and are not repeated:

- `docs/isaiah-jeremiah-ezra-five-languages-terms.md`: the Ezra section is the
  base for Nehemiah (God of heaven, house of God, returned exiles, temple
  servants, Persian titles, letters).
- `docs/kings-chronicles-five-languages-terms.md` and
  `docs/samuel-five-languages-terms.md`: address forms at court, the king's
  officials, eunuchs, gatekeepers and singers, the temple refrain.
- `docs/psalms-proverbs-five-languages-terms.md`: *ḥesed*, Sheol, the fear of
  the LORD, the fool, God's *torah*, poetry lines, prayer forms.
- `docs/pentateuch-fr-es-sw-terms.md`, `docs/pentateuch-vietnamese-korean-terms.md`
  and `docs/genesis-vietnamese-korean-terms.md`: names of God, measures,
  address between husband and wife, parent and child, master and servant.
- The style guides: `docs/french-style.md`, `docs/swahili-style.md`,
  `docs/vietnamese-style.md`, `docs/korean-style.md`, and for Spanish
  `docs/nt-spanish-swahili-terms.md` with the Spanish already in `content/`.

The English choices are in `docs/ezra-job-terms.md` (Nehemiah, Esther, Job)
and `docs/ecclesiastes-song-terms.md` (Ecclesiastes and Song of Songs, with
the founder's rulings of 2026-10-16). Every form below was checked against
the language's published text; where they differed, the published form won
(evidence in brackets, † = already published).

If a choice here changes, every passage drafted under it is revised.
Decision text stays in English, quoting the words of the language.

## All five languages

- Translate from the Hebrew, using the literal gloss, the English and the
  meaning decisions; never from a reference Bible. These pull hardest: Job
  1–2, 3, 19:25–27, 28 and 38–42; Ecclesiastes 1:2–11, 3:1–8 and 12:1–8; Song
  2:7, 2:10–13 and 8:6–7; Esther 4:14; Nehemiah 1:5–11 and 9. Draft from the
  gloss and rewrite any line that matches a well-known version for more than
  a short common phrase. Ecclesiastes 3:1–8, Esther 4:14 and the Song's
  refrain are drafted fresh in each language; the wordings below are
  starting points, not quotations.
- **Lines follow the English record**, level by level, with breaks placed at
  the language's own phrase boundaries (`docs/english-style.md` section 10):
  - Job: every record from 3:1 to 42:6 is set in `\q` lines at L, B and R. The
    speech introductions ("And Job answered and said:"; 32:1–5; 38:1; 40:1, 3,
    6; 42:1) are prose. Where an English record joins a prose introduction
    and lines (1:21; 32:6), the language does the same.
  - Ecclesiastes: only the records whose English has `\q` (about 60: the
    poems 1:4–11, 3:1–8, 12:1–7, and the proverb clusters of 7, 10 and 11).
    The rest is prose.
  - Song of Songs: every record except 1:1.
  - Nehemiah and Esther: prose, including the prayer of Nehemiah 9.
- **Speech.** Job's speeches, God's speeches, Qoheleth's words and the voices
  of the Song take no quotation marks; words quoted inside them do (Job 4:17,
  Song 2:10; 5:2). The prose of Job 1–2 and 42:7–17, Nehemiah and Esther quote
  speech as narrative does. Long speeches run across verses without closing
  the quotation at each verse.
- **Parallels: reuse the language's published wording where the Hebrew is the
  same, and change only what the Hebrew changes.** The later passage's
  rendering decision names each difference.
  - **Nehemiah 7:6–73 is Ezra 2:1–70**, published in all five. Copy each
    language's Ezra 2 record at each level and change only the names and
    numbers the Hebrew changes (Hariph for Jorah, Gibeon for Gibbar, the
    different totals). The Hebrew of Nehemiah has no line for the horses and
    mules of Ezra 2:66, so Hebrew 7:68–72 is English 7:69–73 (the English
    decisions say so). Nehemiah 7:65 is Ezra 2:63 (the Tirshatha).
  - Nehemiah 11:3–19 runs beside 1 Chronicles 9:2–17 (published): take the
    names and the list formula from there.
  - **Nehemiah 9** retells Genesis to Kings. Reuse the published formulas it
    echoes: Exodus 34:6 for "gracious and compassionate, slow to anger and
    great in steadfast love" (9:17, 31; forms below); Deuteronomy 7:9 and
    10:17 for "the great, mighty and awesome God, who keeps covenant and
    steadfast love" (1:5; 9:32); Genesis 11:31 and 17:5 for Ur of the
    Chaldeans and Abraham (9:7); Exodus 15:4 for the Sea of Reeds (9:9; L
    "Sea of Reeds", B and R "Red Sea"); Numbers 21 for Sihon king of Heshbon
    and Og king of Bashan (9:22). Nehemiah 13:1–2 quotes Deuteronomy 23:4–6;
    13:26 recalls 1 Kings 11.
  - Esther 2:6 recalls 2 Kings 24:14–15 (Jeconiah); Esther 3:1 points to Agag
    (1 Samuel 15). Job 12:21 and 24 are Psalm 107:40; Job 3 is near Jeremiah
    20:14–18. Ecclesiastes reuses Proverbs' published words (fool, sluggard,
    fear of God). The Song's words for love and the body follow Proverbs 5:19
    and 7:18.
  - New Testament: Job 5:13 is quoted in 1 Corinthians 3:19 and Job 41:3
    (Hebrew numbering) in Romans 11:35; James 5:11 names Job. Keep the key
    words so a reader sees the quotation; do not copy the Greek-based wording.
- **Settings** (exact forms in each language section):
  - `divine_name` on every YHWH. Esther has none and Ecclesiastes has none
    (only *Elohim*): no span, and God is never added to Esther at any level.
    The Song has one, possibly: 8:6 at L only, the short form *Yah*. In Job the
    name stands only in the prologue and epilogue, in the speech headings of
    38:1, 40:1, 40:3, 40:6 and 42:1, and in 12:9. *El*, *Eloah* and *Elohim*
    are "God", *Shaddai* "the Almighty" and *Adonai* (28:28) "the Lord": none
    takes a `divine_name` span.
  - `deity_pronoun` where the English has one and the language has a free
    pronoun (Korean none). Job's Redeemer (19:25) has one in English and takes
    one; the Song has none.
  - `units` only where the English converts: Nehemiah 3:13; 5:15; 7:70–71;
    10:33; Esther 3:9; 5:14; 7:9; Job 42:11. Literal keeps the unit. Metric
    values, from the English conversion:

    | Verse | Unit | Value |
    |---|---|---|
    | Neh 3:13 | 1,000 cubits | about 450 m |
    | Neh 5:15 | forty shekels of silver | about 460 g of silver |
    | Neh 7:70 | 2,200 minas of silver | about 1,250 kg (1.25 t) of silver |
    | Neh 7:71 | 2,000 minas of silver | about 1,140 kg (1.1 t) of silver |
    | Neh 10:33 | a third of a shekel | about 4 g of silver |
    | Esth 3:9 | ten thousand talents of silver | about 340 tonnes of silver |
    | Esth 5:14; 7:9 | fifty cubits | about 23 m |
    | Job 42:11 | a *qesitah* | "a piece of money" (no metric value) |

    Darics (Neh 7:69–71) stay darics at every level, as in Ezra.
  - Spanish `plural_you` for every plural "you": Nehemiah's speeches to the
    people and nobles, the prayer of 9 where it addresses "you" (God is
    singular), Job's replies to his three friends, Elihu to the three, God to
    Eliphaz and his friends (42:7–8), the Song's addresses to the women of
    Jerusalem and the friends.
  - `gender` only where the language really has two wordings. Ecclesiastes
    has about 137 English gender spans ("a person / a man", "their / his"):
    French and Spanish follow their rules; Swahili, Vietnamese and Korean
    rarely need one.
- **Numbers.** Figures for numbers above ninety-nine and for every list total
  (2,172; 127 provinces), as Ezra. Days and months stay in words.
- Rendering decisions: zero to two per sentence, only where the language had
  to choose. A book-wide choice is recorded once per block of chapters (its
  first use); later uses follow it.

## Terms and names each language settles

Each language section below gives its wording, at L / B / R where they
differ, for:

- **Nehemiah**: the God of heaven; the house of God; the returned exiles;
  temple servants, gatekeepers, singers; governor (*pechah*) and Tirshatha;
  nobles and officials; the wall and the gates of Jerusalem; "Remember me, O
  my God, for good"; the book of the law of Moses; the festival of Booths; the
  firm agreement and the sealed document (10:1, English 9:38); the formula of
  9:17; cupbearer; the forms of address at court and in the memoir.
- **Esther**: Susa the citadel; the king's gate; satraps, governors,
  officials; eunuchs; the harem; queen; the signet ring; the gold scepter;
  the laws of Persia and Media; Pur and Purim; the gallows; "from another
  place"; "for such a time as this"; the 127 provinces from India to Cush;
  the Jews.
- **Job**: the satan / the Accuser; the sons of God; blameless and upright;
  fearing God and turning from evil; the euphemistic "bless" = curse; the
  speech introductions; *Shaddai*, *Eloah*, *El* beside YHWH; Sheol and
  Abaddon; the Redeemer (19:25); Leviathan, Behemoth, Rahab; wisdom and its
  place (28); the storm (38:1); dust and ashes (42:6); the stars of 9:9 and
  38:31–32.
- **Ecclesiastes**: Qoheleth / the Teacher; *hevel*; "Breath of breaths";
  under the sun; chasing the wind; *yitron*; *ʿamal*; *ḥeleq*; *ʿolam*
  (3:11); fear God; *ruaḥ* as life-breath; the frame of 3:1–8.
- **Song of Songs**: *dodi*, *raʿyati*, "my beautiful one", "my dove"; *dodim*
  and *ʾahavah*; my sister, my bride; the daughters of Jerusalem; the
  Shulammite; the refrain (2:7; 3:5; 8:4); 8:6; register of the body; how each
  level shows who speaks.
- **Address forms**, chiefly Vietnamese and Korean: king and subjects, Esther
  and the king, Job and his friends, Job and God, God and Job, God and the
  Accuser, the Teacher and the reader, the lovers.

Notes on the table:
- Joiakim the high priest (Neh 12) is not King Jehoiakim; keep the short
  Hebrew form apart from Joïaqim / Joacim / Yehoyakimu / Giê-hô-gia-kim / 여호야김.
- "Agagite" keeps the published form of Agag (A-ga, 아각) so the link the
  meaning decision at Esther 3:1 points to stays visible.
- Swahili avoids *Mbuzi* for "the Buzite" (it reads "goat"): *wa ukoo wa
  Buzi*, with a decision.
- Published spellings that disagree, now settled for these books: Spanish

## Korean

Samuel, Psalms, Kings and Isaiah–Ezra notes hold. Every form below was checked

**Settings recap**
- Every YHWH is `{{divine_name:주|야훼}}` with the particle outside the span
  (께서, 의, 가, 를, 께, 여); Lord GOD is 주 `{{divine_name:하나님|야훼}}`; Yah
  is `{{divine_name:주|야}}`. A bare 주 never stands for YHWH; 여호와 is never
  used. Adonai alone is 주님.
- **No `deity_pronoun` spans.** God in the third person is 그분, with -시- and
  께서; God speaking names himself without -시-.
- `gender`: `{{gender:자녀들|아들들}}`, `{{gender:사람의 자녀들|사람의 아들들}}`,
  `{{gender:형제자매|형제}}`; 사람, 누구든지 need none.
- `units`: `{{units:약 X|unit}}`, with the particle repeated inside both
  options when one attaches [Ezra 2:69 `{{units:약 2.9톤과|5,000마네와}}`]:
  `{{units:약 450미터|1,000규빗}}`, `{{units:약 340톤|10,000달란트}}`,
  `{{units:약 23미터|50규빗}}`, `{{units:약 1.25톤|2,200마네}}`. Always 약,
  never X쯤. Job 42:11 `{{units:돈 한 닢|크시타 하나}}`. Figures for years,
  ages and large numbers (127개 지방, 2,172명); months and days in words (아달 월
  십삼일).

  memoir keeps 나 in the plain style, as Ezra's.
- **King and subjects**: subjects speak 합쇼체 with 임금님 (내 주 임금님) and
  call themselves 임금님의 종 / 이 종 (L, B) or 저 (R); the king speaks 해라체
  with 너, 너희 [Samuel note]. Nehemiah before Artaxerxes likewise.
- **Esther and the king**: Esther speaks 합쇼체 with 임금님 and 저 at every
  level, in the court's third person (임금님께서 좋게 여기시면). The king
  speaks to the queen in 하오체 (에스더 왕후, 무슨 일이오? 그대의 소청이
  무엇이오?), to Haman and his servants in 해라체. Mordecai to Esther 해라체;
  Esther to Mordecai 해요체, as a daughter to her father. Haman and Zeresh
