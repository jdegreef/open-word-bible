# Russian terms: Genesis to Revelation

**Status: proposed, awaiting native Russian reviewers.** Every Russian record
is `ai_draft`. If a choice here changes, every passage drafted under it is
revised.

## How this glossary relates to the style guide

`docs/russian-style.md` is the brief for drafting Russian and still holds in
full: the three levels, forms of address, spelling (ё), typography, the
reader settings, the New Testament's words (section 6) and the conventions
settled in Matthew (section 7b). This glossary is the single reference for
the words and formulas the Old Testament adds, and it settles section 7a
(the divine name, poetry lines) for Genesis to Malachi. It replaces four
notes written book group by book group
(`docs/pentateuch-hindi-russian-terms.md`,
`docs/history-hindi-russian-terms.md`, `docs/poetry-hindi-russian-terms.md`,
`docs/prophets-hindi-russian-terms.md`); where a later note changed an
earlier one, only the later rule is given here, with what it replaced.
`docs/hindi-terms.md` is its Hindi counterpart. Each book's own terms note
(`docs/isaiah-terms.md` and the others) still settles the meaning; this file
settles only the Russian wording.

Where an earlier book or the New Testament in this translation already has a
form, that form wins. Decision text stays in English, quoting the Russian
words.

## Drafting

- Translate from the Hebrew (and the Aramaic in Daniel 2:4-7:28 and
  Jeremiah 10:11), using the literal gloss, the English and the meaning
  decisions to see how a sentence was read. Never from a reference Bible,
  and never reproduce a copyrighted Bible's wording (the style guide names
  them). The familiar passages pull hardest toward the Synodal: Genesis 1-3,
  Exodus 20, Deuteronomy 6, Numbers 6:24-26; Psalms 23, 51, 91, 103,
  Proverbs 3:5-6, Proverbs 31, Ecclesiastes 1-3 and 12; Isaiah 9, 40, 53,
  Jeremiah 31 and Micah 5. Draft from the gloss and rewrite any sentence
  that matches a well-known version for more than a short common phrase.
- Keep every `\v N ` marker where the English has it. Lines (`\q `): see
  [Poetry conventions](#poetry-conventions).
- Quotation marks as in the New Testament: « » outside and “ ” inside, never
  „. Each level opens and closes the same quotations as the English level.
- Settings mirror the English spans where Russian has the same choice:
  `divine_name`, `deity_pronoun` (as in the New Testament), `units`, and
  `gender` where Russian really has two wordings (люди, человек, кто need
  none, as in the New Testament).
- Narrative: perfective past (сказал, пошёл), imperfective for what went on
  or kept happening.
- Laws and instructions (Exodus 20-40, Leviticus, much of Numbers,
  Deuteronomy's speeches): imperative or future at L and B (`не убивай`,
  `пусть принесёт`, `он принесёт`); `нельзя`, `должен` at R.
- Rendering decisions: zero to two per sentence, only where Russian had to
  choose. A book-wide choice is recorded once, in the first passage file of
  each book; later chapters follow it.
- Measures: metric by default where the English converts, the original unit
  as the alternative; Literal keeps the original unit where the English
  Literal does. Where the English has no `units` span, neither does the
  Russian. Use the English conversion's values: cubit 45 cm (handbreadth
  7.5 cm), shekel 11.5 g, beka 5.7 g, gerah 0.6 g, talent 34 kg, mina
  0.6 kg, ephah 22 litres (a tenth 2.2 litres), omer 2.2 litres, seah
  7 litres, hin 3.7 litres, homer and cor 220 litres. Round as the English
  rounds. Write a decimal comma and a thin no-break space in numbers of five
  digits or more (603 550), as Revelation does. Days, years, ages and counts
  are not units. Ezekiel's temple measurements (40-48) take `units` spans
  where the English has them.

## Address and pronouns

- **ты to God and from God**, as in the New Testament, at every level. The
  Hebrew singular and plural "you" come through as ты and вы: вы only for a
  Hebrew plural.
- **`deity_pronoun` spans** on every pronoun for God: `{{deity_pronoun:он|Он}}`,
  and `{{deity_pronoun:я|Я}}` mid-sentence too (style guide section 7 has
  the full list). None on the reflexives свой and себя, none on a
  sentence-initial Я (written plainly with its capital), and none on anyone
  else. The Servant's "my" when God speaks (мой раб) takes the span.
- **Courtly address is in the words, not in вы**: господин to Pharaoh;
  господин мой царь to the king, with ты (as the style guide). The psalmist
  says ты to the king, to people and to his enemies; Job's friends and Job
  say ты; the lovers in the Song of Songs say ты.
- **Lines the New Testament quotes keep its forms of address**, even where
  they bend a rule.

## Recurring formulas

### Kings and Chronicles: the regnal formulas

One wording per level through Kings and Chronicles. B smooths; R may split
the sentence.

- **Accession.** "In the Nth year of X king of Judah, Y son of Z became king
  over Israel in Samaria, and he reigned N years." L: В N-й год X, царя
  Иудейского, воцарился Y, сын Z, над Израилем в Самарии и царствовал N
  лет.
- **Age and mother (Judah).** Ему было N лет, когда он воцарился, и N лет он
  царствовал в Иерусалиме; имя его матери — X.
- **Verdict.** "He did what was evil / right in the eyes of the LORD": он
  делал то, что было злом / правильным в глазах `{{divine_name:ГОСПОДА|Яхве}}`
  (L, B); что не нравилось / нравилось `{{divine_name:ГОСПОДУ|Яхве}}` (R).
- **Source.** "Are they not written in the book of the chronicles of the
  kings of Israel?": Разве это не записано в книге летописи царей Израиля?
- **Death.** "He slept with his fathers and was buried": почил со своими
  отцами (L, B), умер (R).
- **Genealogies (1 Chronicles 1-9)** keep one list formula per level ("the
  sons of X: A, B and C"; "X fathered Y", родил at L, as Matthew 1), and
  every name in the forms of Genesis, Numbers and the name tables below.

## Poetry conventions

- **Lines follow the English record.** Where an English level has `\q`
  breaks, that level is set in lines too, with the breaks at Russian's own
  phrase boundaries; where the English level is prose, so is the Russian.
  This settles style guide section 7a, which asked for lines at every level.
  Pentateuch poems in lines where the English has them: Genesis 49, Exodus
  15, Numbers 23-24, Deuteronomy 32-33. Jonah 2 and Habakkuk 3 are poetry;
  Jonah 1, 3-4 and Haggai are prose.
- **Acrostics** (Lamentations, and the acrostic psalms): follow the English;
  no letter headings unless it has them.
- **Personified women.** Wisdom and Folly are women in the Hebrew; мудрость
  is feminine, so the verbs and pronouns follow. Zion and Jerusalem pictured
  as a woman take feminine forms, against the grammatical gender, as
  Lamentations.

## Names

**Policy.** Names that occur in the New Testament keep the New Testament form
in this translation (`grep` the name in content/MAT, LUK, ACT, ROM, HEB, JAS,
REV in Russian). Keep a list for the book and use one form throughout. Other
names follow the established Russian Bible form (the Synodal transcriptions
are traditional names, not copyrighted wording), and otherwise transcribe the
Hebrew. Each prophet's name and his father's take the New Testament or
earlier forms (or Ezra's).

- **Two names for one person.** Keep each name where the Hebrew has it.
  Uzziah is also called Azariah in Kings (Азария). Jeremiah writes both
  Nebuchadnezzar and Nebuchadrezzar: Навуходоносор and Навуходорносор, each
  where the Hebrew has it (Ezekiel has the second); elsewhere Навуходоносор.
- Beersheba Беэр-Шева is kept apart from Bathsheba Вирсавия.

## Parallel passages

**Policy.** Draft once, reuse. Where the Hebrew is the same, the later
passage copies the earlier wording at each level; where it differs, the
wording differs, with a decision naming the other passage. A rendering
decision is added only where Russian made its own choice. Samuel and Kings
are drafted before Chronicles, which retells them. Where both sides are
drafted at once, the later passage copies the earlier once both exist; the
consistency pass checks.

## The New Testament quotes the Old

**Policy.** Where the New Testament quotes a line and the Hebrew says the
same, use the New Testament's Russian wording at the matching level (grep the
quoting book). Where the New Testament follows the Greek Old Testament and
differs, translate the Hebrew and add a decision naming the New Testament
form: Psalm 40:6 "a body", 68:18 "gave gifts", 102:25-27 in part, Proverbs
3:34 "the proud", Isaiah 7:14 "virgin", 40:3 punctuation, 61:1 "recovery of
sight", Amos 9:11-12 in Acts 15, Joel 2:28-32 small differences. A rendering
may not narrow the meaning decision. Quoted lines keep the New Testament's
forms of address.

## Book notes

- **Genesis to Deuteronomy.** The first Old Testament books drafted in
  Russian. Courtly address to Pharaoh is carried by господин, not by вы.
  Exodus 3:14-15 and 6:2-3 follow the English on the name.
- **Joshua to Ruth.** Joshua is Иисус Навин (Иисус, сын Навина); *shofet* in
  Judges is судья.
- **1-2 Samuel.** Forms first used in 2 Samuel are for Kings, Chronicles and
  Psalms to reuse (names above). "How the mighty have fallen" (1:19, 25,
  27): Как пали сильные! (L), Как пали богатыри! (B), Как пали храбрые воины!
  (R). Address: ты throughout, господин мой царь to the king, вы only for a
  Hebrew plural. 2 Samuel 22 was drafted before Psalm 18 and sets the rock
  and Sheol words.
- **1-2 Kings, 1-2 Chronicles.** Regnal formulas above; Chronicles reuses
  Samuel and Kings; the genealogies' list formula. 1 Chronicles 16:8-36
  (Psalms 105, 96, 106) and 2 Kings 18:13-20:19 (Isaiah 36-39) were drafted
  here first and the later books reuse them.
- **Job.** "The satan" (Literal) / "the Accuser" (Readable): сатана (L),
  Обвинитель (B, R), as the English level has a title or a name. "The sons
  of God": сыны Божьи (L), небесные существа (B, R), as the English level.
- **Psalms.** Titles, Selah and parallels above; Psalm 119's eight words.
- **Proverbs.**

  | Hebrew (English) | Russian |
  |---|---|
  | *ḥokmah* (wisdom) | мудрость |
  | *binah*, *tevunah* (understanding) | разум (L, B); понимание (R) |
  | *daʿat* (knowledge) | знание |
  | *musar* (discipline; correction at R) | наставление (L, B); вразумление (R) |
  | *torah* of father or mother (teaching) | наставление |
  | *kesil*, *ʾewil* (fool) | глупец |
  | *lets* (mocker) | насмешник |
  | *peti* (the simple / naive / easily fooled) | простак (L), наивный (B), легковерный (R) |
  | *ʿatsel* (sluggard) | ленивец |
  | *beni* (my son) | сын мой |
  | the strange woman (as the English level: foreign / forbidden / another man's wife) | чужая жена; R as the English |

- **Ecclesiastes.** Qoheleth: Когелет (L), Учитель (B, R), as the English
  level. *hevel* follows the English level: дуновение / пар (L, the image),
  тщетно, мимолётно (B, R), never the Synodal's суета сует. "Under the sun":
  под солнцем at every level.
- **Song of Songs.** The lovers say ты. The speakers follow the English
  level's marks. Translated as love poetry; an allegorical reading is not
  added.
- **Isaiah.** "The Holy One of Israel" one form. The Servant passages
  (42:1-9; 49:1-13; 50:4-11; 52:13-53:12) drafted with care: мой раб, мой
  слуга in 42:1-4. 7:14 *ʿalmah*: the meaning decision is "the young woman",
  so девушка at every level, with a footnoted decision naming Matthew 1:23's
  дева (from the Greek *parthenos*).
- **Jeremiah.** Baruch, the Rechabites and the kings of Judah as Kings;
  Nebuchadnezzar and Nebuchadrezzar each as the Hebrew has it; "the LORD our
  righteousness" one form. Aramaic in 10:11.
- **Lamentations.** Acrostics as the English; "daughter Zion" one form; Zion
  as a woman takes feminine forms.
- **Ezekiel.** "Son of man" as above at every address; "they will know that I
  am the LORD" one wording per level; the temple measurements (40-48) with
  `units` spans where the English has them, and the furnishings as 1 Kings 7.
- **Daniel.** Aramaic 2:4-7:28. Names above (settled once). "The Ancient of
  Days" Ветхий днями (L, B); "the abomination of desolation" мерзость
  запустения, as Matthew 24:15 (11:31 L); "the time of the end" (12:4 L:
  время конца); "peoples, nations and languages" племена, народы и языки.
- **The Twelve.** Each prophet's name as the New Testament or Ezra. Hosea's
  hesed is Israel's loyalty (above). Jonah 2 and Habakkuk 3 are poetry; Jonah
  1, 3-4 and Haggai are prose. Malachi 3:1 and 4:5-6 (Hebrew 3:23-24) keep
  the Gospels' wording where the Hebrew agrees.
- **New Testament.** `docs/russian-style.md` sections 6 and 7b and the book
  terms notes.

## Settled while drafting

Decisions recorded while the blocks were harmonised, kept here as they were
made (each is also in its topic section above).

- **Torah** in the Prophets: наставление at every level; Jeremiah 31:33
  keeps Hebrews 8:10's "law" with a decision.
- **Woe** at Readable: «Беда ждёт тех, кто…».
- **The Servant** (Isaiah 42-53): мой раб, but мой слуга inside 42:1-4,
  which Matthew 12:18-21 quotes.
- **Redeemer** (*goʾel*): Искупитель.
- **Mighty One of Jacob**: Сильный Иаковлев (Genesis 49:24, Psalm 132).
- **Zion and Jerusalem pictured as a woman** take feminine forms, against the
  grammatical gender, as Lamentations.
- **Hesed as Israel's loyalty to God** (Hosea 4:1, 6:4, 6:6, 10:12, 12:7):
  неизменная любовь (L), преданная любовь (B, R); Hosea 6:6 keeps Matthew
  9:13's frame with a footnoted decision.
- **Isaiah 7:14**: девушка, with a footnoted decision naming дева.
- **Daniel**: "peoples, nations and languages" follows the nations / peoples
  rule (племена, народы и языки); "the Ancient of Days" Ветхий днями (L, B).
- **God's hesed at R**: неизменная любовь, replacing the Pentateuch's
  "верная любовь or милость".
