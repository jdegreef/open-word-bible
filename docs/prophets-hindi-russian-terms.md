# Hindi and Russian: the Prophets (Isaiah to Malachi)

**Status: proposed, awaiting native Hindi and Russian reviewers.** These are
the choices used to draft Isaiah, Jeremiah, Lamentations, Ezekiel, Daniel and
the Twelve in Hindi and Russian, so that blocks drafted in parallel read as
one translation. The earlier notes still hold in full:
`docs/pentateuch-hindi-russian-terms.md`, `docs/history-hindi-russian-terms.md`
and `docs/poetry-hindi-russian-terms.md` (lines, names of God, hesed, Sheol,
address, the "nations / peoples" rule). Where an earlier book or the New
Testament already has a form, that form wins. Decision text stays in English,
quoting the Hindi or Russian words.

## Both languages

- Translate from the Hebrew (and Aramaic in Daniel 2:4-7:28 and Jeremiah
  10:11). Never from a reference Bible; Isaiah 9, 40, 53, Jeremiah 31 and
  Micah 5 pull hardest toward the Hindi Old Version and the Synodal: draft from
  the gloss.
- Lines (`\q`) exactly as the English level has them.
- The prophet's own name and his father's take the New Testament or earlier
  forms (grep): Isaiah यशायाह / Исаия, Jeremiah यिर्मयाह / Иеремия, Daniel
  दानिय्येल / Даниил (Matthew 24:15), Jonah योना / Иона, and so on.

## The New Testament quotes these books

Where a line is quoted and the Hebrew says the same, use the New
Testament's wording at the matching level (grep the quoting book). Where the
New Testament follows the Greek Old Testament (Isaiah 7:14 "virgin", 40:3
punctuation, 61:1 "recovery of sight", Amos 9:11-12 in Acts 15, Joel 2:28-32
small differences), translate the Hebrew and add a decision naming the New
Testament form. The main quotations: Isaiah 6:9-10; 7:14; 8:14; 9:1-2; 11:10;
22:13; 25:8; 28:11-12, 16; 29:13-14; 40:3-5, 6-8; 42:1-4; 45:23; 49:6;

65:1-2; 66:1-2; Jeremiah 7:11; 9:24; 31:15, 31-34; Hosea 1:10; 2:23; 6:6;

## Book notes

- **Isaiah.** "The Holy One of Israel" one form; the Servant passages
  (42:1-9; 49:1-13; 50:4-11; 52:13-53:12) drafted with care. Cyrus कुस्रू /
  Кир (as Ezra). Isaiah 7:14 *ʿalmah*: Hindi कुँवारी / Russian дева as the
  Hebrew allows, with a decision on Matthew 1:23 and the English level's
  choice.
- **Jeremiah.** Baruch, the Rechabites, the kings of Judah as Kings;
  Nebuchadnezzar (Jeremiah also writes Nebuchadrezzar: keep each as the
  Hebrew has it, one form each); "the LORD our righteousness".
- **Lamentations.** Acrostics: follow the English (no letter headings unless
  it has them). "Daughter Zion" one form.
- **Ezekiel.** "Son of man" as above at every address; "they will know that I
  am the LORD" one wording per level; the temple measurements (40-48) with
  units spans where the English has them, and the furnishings as 1 Kings 7.
- **Daniel.** Aramaic 2:4-7:28. Belteshazzar, Shadrach, Meshach,
  Abednego, Nebuchadnezzar, Belshazzar, Darius the Mede: settle once.
  "The Ancient of Days", "the abomination of desolation" (as Matthew 24:15),
  "the time of the end".
- **The Twelve.** Each prophet's name as the New Testament or Ezra. Jonah's
  psalm (2) and Habakkuk 3 are poetry; Jonah 1, 3-4 and Haggai are prose.
  Malachi 3:1 and 4:5-6 (Hebrew 3:23-24) keep the Gospels' wording where the
  Hebrew agrees.
