# John: recurring terms and book-wide choices

**Status: proposed, awaiting the founder's approval.** Drafting follows these
choices so that the whole Gospel is consistent; if a choice changes, every
passage drafted under it is revised. Counts are occurrences in John in the
SBLGNT (MACULA data). Read with `docs/english-style.md`; the settled English
for key terms is enforced through `data/key_terms.json`.

## Words with one English rendering

| Term (count) | English, every level | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| believe (98) | believe / believe in | The verb is a key term. John never uses the noun "faith"; keep the verb even at Readable ("trust" only with a decision). "Believe in" for the construction with *eis*. |
| world (78) | the world | Key term. A meaning decision says which sense: the created order (1:10a), humanity (3:16), or humanity organised against God (15:18). |
| eternal life (17) | eternal life | Key term (*aiōnios*). Readable keeps the phrase and may explain it once per passage ("the life of the age to come, which starts now"). |
| sign (17) | sign | Key term. Never "miracle": John's word points beyond the deed. |
| glorify (23) | glorify | Key term, with "glory". The "hour" of glorification is the cross (12:23; 17:1). |
| Son of Man | the Son of Man | A title; never under the gender setting. Capitalised. |
| Rabbi (8) | Rabbi | John translates it himself (1:38); keep the word. |
| lifted up (5) | lifted up | Every level keeps the double sense: raised on the cross and exalted (3:14; 8:28; 12:32-34). A meaning decision explains it; Readable may add "on the cross" with a rendering decision. |

## Choices made in context

**"The Jews" (*hoi Ioudaioi*, 71).** The most sensitive decision in the book.
John uses the word in several senses: the Jewish people (4:9, 4:22 "salvation
is from the Jews"), people of Judea (11:19), and very often the religious
authorities in Jerusalem who oppose Jesus (1:19, 5:16-18, 9:22). Every
occurrence gets a meaning decision saying which sense it has.
- Literal: always "the Jews", so readers see the word John wrote.
- Balanced: "the Jewish leaders" (or "the Jewish authorities") where the
  context shows the authorities; "the Jews" / "Jewish" where it means the
  people.
- Readable: as Balanced, naming the group plainly ("the Jewish leaders in
  Jerusalem").
- Jesus, his disciples and his mother are Jews; no rendering may suggest
  otherwise.

**"Truly, truly" (*amēn amēn*, 25 times as a double).** Literal "Truly, truly
I say to you"; Balanced "Truly, truly, I tell you"; Readable "I tell you the
truth". One rendering decision per passage, not per occurrence.

**Remain (*menō*, 40).** "Remain" in the theological sense (6:56; 15:4-10:
"remain in me"), not the archaic "abide". In ordinary senses use the natural
word ("stay", 1:38-39). A meaning decision marks where John plays on both
senses (1:38-39 with 15:4).

**Hour (*hōra*, 26).** "Hour" at Literal and Balanced when it means Jesus's
appointed time (2:4; 7:30; 12:23; 13:1; 17:1), so the thread stays visible;
Readable may say "my time". Clock times stay literal ("about the tenth hour",
1:39), with Balanced and Readable giving the modern time ("about four in the
afternoon") and a note.

**"I am" (*egō eimi*).** With a predicate ("I am the bread of life") it is
ordinary. Without one (8:24, 8:28, 8:58; 13:19; 18:5-8) a meaning decision
says that it echoes God's name (Exodus 3:14; Isaiah 43:10) and how sure that
is in the context; 8:58 keeps "before Abraham was, I am" at every level. In
18:5-8 Literal and Balanced say "I am he" only if the decision finds the
plain sense uppermost; the double sense is noted either way.

**The Paraclete (*paraklētos*, 4).** "the Advocate" at every level: John's
context is testimony and judgment (15:26; 16:8-11). A footnote gives
"Helper", "Counselor", "Comforter".

**Spirit (*pneuma*, 24).** Capital "Spirit" when it is the Holy Spirit; this
is a meaning decision each time. 3:8 plays on wind/spirit (one word in Greek):
note the pun, render "wind" and "Spirit". 4:24 "God is spirit" lowercase.

**The two verbs for love.** *agapaō* (37) and *phileō* (13) are both "love".
John uses both of the Father's love for the Son (3:35; 5:20), so the
alternation in 21:15-17 is most likely style; the decision there is marked
uncertain and footnoted, giving the view that a distinction is meant.

**Messiah (*Messias*, 2).** 1:41 and 4:25 have the Hebrew title in Greek
letters, then John translates it as Christos. Write "Messiah" plainly (no
setting), and give the explanation its own alternative so no setting can
produce "Messiah (which means Messiah)":
"(which means {{christos:Christ|Anointed One}})", as 1:41 does.

**Christ with the article.** Where the Greek says "the Christ" as a title
("I am not the Christ", 1:20), every level writes `the {{christos:Christ|Messiah}}`,
since "I am not Christ" would misread it as a name.

**Temple words.** *hieron* is the whole temple area: "temple" at Literal,
"temple courts" or "temple area" at Balanced and Readable. *naos* is the
sanctuary itself: "temple" at every level, so that "destroy this temple ...
the temple of his body" (2:19-21) stays clear.

**Believe, entrust (2:24).** The key term "believe" also means "entrust";
where John plays on the two (2:23-24), a word_sense meaning decision says so
and excuses the key term.

**Jesus's brothers (2:12; 7:3-10).** No gender setting: specific men are
meant. A footnote gives the views of who they were (younger brothers, half
brothers, cousins).

**Historic present.** John often tells past events in the present ("he says",
1:21). Literal keeps the present; Balanced and Readable use the past. One
rendering decision per passage, not per verb.

**Footnotes on "the Jews".** Footnote the occurrences where Balanced and
Readable depart from the word (the authorities); the ones that mean the
people need a meaning decision but no footnote.

**"Woman" as an address (2:4; 19:26; 20:15).** Respectful in Greek, not
cold. Literal "Woman"; Balanced "Woman" with a note, or "Dear woman" with a
rendering decision; Readable "Mother" only at 2:4 and 19:26 where she is his
mother, with a decision.

**"Sir" or "Lord" (*kyrie* as an address).** People who do not yet know who
Jesus is say "Sir" at every level (the Samaritan woman, 4:11-19; the
official, 4:49), with a footnote giving "Lord" the first time in a passage.
His disciples, and those who have come to believe, say "Lord" (6:68; 11:27;
20:28). The narrator's "the Lord" (4:1; 20:20) is always "the Lord".

**Where a speech ends.** The Greek has no quotation marks. Where it is unclear
whether the speaker's words go on or the narrator comments (3:16-21 after
Jesus; 3:31-36 after John the Baptist), a punctuation meaning decision, marked
uncertain and footnoted, says where the quotation closes; both passages keep
the speech running to the end of the chapter section.

**The beloved disciple.** Always "the disciple whom Jesus loved"; never named.

**Disciples.** "disciples" at every level, Readable included: the word is
plain enough and recurs throughout the Gospel.

## Disputed passages

- **7:53-8:11** (the woman caught in adultery) is in the SBLGNT. The oldest
  manuscripts do not have it and later ones place it in different spots. It
  is translated in [square brackets] at every level, opened by a
  `text_variant` meaning decision with a footnote, as the charter does for
  Mark 16:9-20.
- **5:3b-4** (the angel stirring the water) is not in the SBLGNT text. A
  footnote at 5:3 gives it, and the verse numbering skips 5:4 as the source
  does.
- **1:34**: the SBLGNT reads "the Chosen One of God"; "the Son of God" is the
  variant and goes in the footnote.
- Other variants follow `docs/english-style.md`: the SBLGNT governs, and a
  reviewer checks each variant note against the apparatus.
