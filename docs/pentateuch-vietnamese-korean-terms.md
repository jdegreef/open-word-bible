# Exodus to Deuteronomy in Vietnamese and Korean

**Status: proposed, awaiting native Vietnamese and Korean reviewers.** These
are the choices used to draft Exodus, Leviticus, Numbers and Deuteronomy in
Vietnamese and Korean, so that chapters drafted in parallel read as one
translation. `docs/genesis-vietnamese-korean-terms.md` still holds (names of
God, address between people, measures, narrative style, reference Bibles not
to reproduce), as do `docs/vietnamese-style.md` and `docs/korean-style.md`.
The terms below match what Joshua to Kings already use in these languages
(`docs/joshua-ruth-five-languages-terms.md`,
`docs/samuel-five-languages-terms.md`,
`docs/kings-chronicles-five-languages-terms.md`), and
`docs/pentateuch-fr-es-sw-terms.md` lists the same concepts for French,
Spanish and Swahili. If a choice here changes, every passage drafted under it
is revised.

Decision text stays in English, quoting the Vietnamese or Korean words.

## Both languages

- Translate from the Hebrew, using the literal gloss, the English and the
  meaning decisions to see how a sentence was read; never from a reference
  Bible. Familiar passages (Exodus 3, 12, 14–15, 20; Leviticus 19;
  Numbers 6:24–26; Deuteronomy 6, 30) pull hardest: draft from the gloss and
  rewrite any line that matches a well-known version for more than a short
  common phrase.
- Keep every `\v N ` marker where the English has it, and each level's own
  `\q ` line breaks where the English level has them (Exodus 15, Numbers
  21:17–18, 23–24, Deuteronomy 32–33).
- Exodus 3:14–15 and 6:2–3, where the name itself is the subject, follow the
  English wording and decisions; elsewhere YHWH is the `divine_name` span of
  the Genesis sheet.
- **Laws and instructions.** Vietnamese: God's commands use *ngươi* / *các
  ngươi* with *phải* and *không được* (L, B; *chớ* only at L where the
  English Literal is terse), and *phải* / *đừng* in plain sentences at R; a
  priest or offerer in the third person is *người ấy* / *người đó*. Korean:
  God's commands in 해라체 with -하라 / -하지 말라 (L, B) and -해야 한다 /
  -하면 안 된다 (R); third-person law in the plain written style (-한다,
  -해야 한다).
- **Moses speaking to Israel** (Deuteronomy, and the speeches in Exodus and
  Numbers): Vietnamese *anh em* for the people and *tôi* for Moses at B and
  R, *các ngươi* / *ngươi* at L where the English Literal keeps the singular
  or plural "you"; Korean 해라체 with 너희 / 너 at L and B, 합쇼체 with 여러분
  at R. The singular and plural "you" of Deuteronomy are kept apart at L as
  the English Literal keeps them.
- **The people speaking to Moses and Aaron**: Vietnamese *ông* / *các ông*
  and *chúng tôi*; Korean 합쇼체 with 당신 / 당신들 and 우리.
- Rendering decisions: zero to two per sentence, only where the language had
  to choose. A book-wide choice is recorded once, in the first file of each
  drafting batch; later chapters just follow it.

## Before review

Only rendering decisions were added; the meaning decisions are the English
records' own. Reviewers should look first at the offering names, the
address forms in the laws and in Moses' speeches, and the decisions marked
uncertain.
