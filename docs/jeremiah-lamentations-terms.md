# Jeremiah and Lamentations: recurring terms and book-wide choices

**Status: proposed, awaiting the founder's approval.** Jeremiah and
Lamentations were drafted chapter by chapter in parallel from one brief. These
are the choices that keep them consistent with each other and with Isaiah,
the prophet already translated. Read with `docs/english-style.md` and
`docs/isaiah-terms.md`, whose table applies here unless this page says
otherwise. The style guide wins where they differ.

## Carried over from Isaiah

The Isaiah table holds for the prophetic formulas and terms: "the LORD of
hosts" / "the LORD of Armies", "declares the LORD" / "says the LORD", "Thus
says the LORD:" / "The LORD says this:" / "This is what the LORD says:",
*hoy* ("Woe to ..."), *bat-tsiyyon* ("daughter of Zion" / "Daughter Zion" /
"the people of Zion"), Sheol, *torah* ("instruction" / "teaching"), the
Chaldeans ("Babylonians" at Readable), the River, the vocative
{{divine_name:LORD|Yahweh}} with no "O", and *hesed* ("loyal love" at
Literal and Balanced, "faithful love" at Readable). Isaiah's speech rule also
holds: the prophet's poetry and God's first-person speech take no quotation
marks; speech quoted inside an oracle takes double quotes; the prose
narratives quote speech as prose does.

**Overlap with other Bibles.** Formulas and name lists count toward the
12-word overlap check like any other wording. The Balanced heading formulas
above are worded to stay clear of it; a name list may stay as it is.

**Names.** Each verse keeps the form its Hebrew uses, with a decision where a
switch could confuse: Nebuchadrezzar and Nebuchadnezzar; Jeconiah, Coniah and
Jehoiachin; Shallum and Jehoahaz (22:11). Names otherwise follow the data's
English forms and the published books (Gedaliah, Baruch son of Neriah,
Ebed-melech the Cushite, Pashhur, Hananiah).

**Prose and poetry.** Jeremiah mixes poetic oracles with prose sermons and
narrative. Poetry is set in lines (`\q`) where the Hebrew accents and
parallelism mark it as verse (most of 2-25 and 30-31, and 46-51); the prose
sermons (7:1-8:3; 11:1-17; 17:19-27; 18:1-12; 21; 25:1-14; 34-36) and the
narrative chapters (26-29; 32-45; 52) are prose. Jeremiah's own laments to God
(11:18-12:6; 15:10-21; 17:14-18; 18:18-23; 20:7-18) are poetry and take a
decision at their start naming them as his prayers.

**Parallels.**
- Jeremiah 52 is 2 Kings 24:18-25:30, and 39:1-10 is 2 Kings 25:1-12, both
  already translated (`content/2KI`). Reuse their wording at every level
  wherever the Hebrew is the same, with an allusion decision naming each
  difference.
- 26:18 quotes Micah 3:12 (not yet translated): note it.
- New Testament uses get brief allusion decisions: 7:11 (Matthew 21:13),
  31:15 (Matthew 2:18), 31:31-34 (Hebrews 8:8-12; 10:16-17), 9:23 (1
  Corinthians 1:31).

**The Greek text.** The Septuagint of Jeremiah is about an eighth shorter than
the Hebrew and places the oracles against the nations (46-51) after 25:13 in a
different order. A footnoted text_variant decision at 1:1 says so once, and
25:13 and 46:1 refer back to it. Other decisions mention a Greek reading only
where it bears on the sense; every such claim is written from general
knowledge and is listed for checking.

**Versification.** Hebrew numbering throughout: Hebrew 8:23 is English 9:1,
and Hebrew 9:1-25 is English 9:2-26. A footnoted decision at 8:23 and 9:1
says so (`data/versification.json`).

## Lamentations

- **Acrostics.** Chapters 1, 2 and 4 are alphabetic acrostics (one verse per
  letter, in order), chapter 3 triples each letter (three verses per letter),
  and chapter 5 has 22 verses but is not an acrostic. A meaning decision at
  the first verse of each chapter says so. Letters are named in Latin
  transliteration (aleph, beth ...), never written in Hebrew script. 2:16-17,
  3:46-51 and 4:16-17 put *pe* before *ayin*; the decision at 2:1 notes it.
- **Poetry.** Every verse is set in lines at every level, following the
  Hebrew's divisions; most verses are the three-line *qinah* (lament) rhythm
  with a long and a short half, which a decision at 1:1 explains.
- ***Ekhah*** ("How ...!", 1:1; 2:1; 4:1): "How" at every level, as an
  exclamation, with a decision that it is the book's Hebrew title.
- **Voices.** The poet, Zion speaking as a woman (1:9c, 11c-22; 2:20-22), and
  the man of chapter 3 take no quotation marks; a decision marks each change
  of speaker. "Daughter Zion" follows Isaiah.
- ***Hesed* in 3:22 and 3:32**: as Isaiah ("loyal love" / "faithful love"),
  with "mercies" (*rachamim*) in 3:22 kept distinct.
