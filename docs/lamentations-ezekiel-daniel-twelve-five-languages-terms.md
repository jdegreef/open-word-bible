# Lamentations, Ezekiel, Daniel and the Twelve in French, Spanish, Swahili, Vietnamese and Korean

**Status: proposed, awaiting native reviewers.** These are the choices used to
draft Lamentations, Ezekiel, Daniel and the twelve minor prophets in five
languages at once, so that chapter blocks drafted in parallel read as one
translation in each language. They continue
`docs/isaiah-jeremiah-ezra-five-languages-terms.md` (prophetic formulas, oracle
punctuation, divine names, address, poetry), which holds here and is not
repeated, and the notes it lists. The English choices are in
`docs/ezekiel-daniel-twelve-terms.md` and, for Lamentations,
`docs/jeremiah-lamentations-terms.md`.

If a choice here changes, every passage drafted under it is revised. Decision
text stays in English, quoting the words of the language. Choices marked
"(uncertain)" are first in line for review.

## All five languages

- Translate from the Hebrew (and the Aramaic of Daniel 2:4b–7:28), using the
  literal gloss, the English and the meaning decisions; never from a reference
  Bible. Lamentations 3:22–23, Ezekiel 36–37, Daniel 7 and 12, Hosea 11,
  Joel 2, Micah 5–6, Habakkuk 2–3, Zechariah 9 and Malachi 3 pull hardest:
  draft from the gloss and rewrite any line that matches a well-known version
  for more than a short common phrase.
- **Lines follow the English record**: where an English level has `\q` lines,
  the language's same level has them, following the Hebrew's division.
- **Ezekiel's "son of man"** (*ben adam*, God's address to the prophet) is
  never the Gospel title: each language keeps a lowercase, plain wording
  (below). Daniel 7:13 "one like a son of man" follows each language's
  Revelation 1:13.
- **Units in Ezekiel 40–48** are worked out on the long cubit the English
  uses: 21 inches, **53 cm**; the reed (six long cubits) **3.2 m**; ephah and
  bath 22 litres, homer and kor 220 litres, hin 3.7 litres (as the
  Pentateuch); each language writes decimals in its own way. Literal keeps
  the original units, as the English does.
- **New Testament quotations** (Daniel 7:13; Hosea 6:6, 11:1; Joel 2:28–32;
  Amos 9:11–12; Jonah 1:17; Micah 5:2; Habakkuk 2:4; Zechariah 9:9, 12:10,
  13:7; Malachi 1:2–3, 3:1, 4:5–6): shared phrases are worded as each
  language's New Testament words them where the meaning is the same; where
  the Hebrew differs from the Greek, a rendering decision says so.
- **Korean particles after a divine-name span.** Where the two options end
  differently (하나님 in a consonant, 야훼 in a vowel), a particle that changes
  form (이/가, 은/는, 을/를, 과/와, 으로/로, 이여/여) goes inside the span with
  each option's own form: `{{divine_name:하나님이|야훼가}}`. Particles that read
  right after both (께서, 께, 의, 도, 만, 에게) stay outside.

### Tenses, voice and speech in these books

- **Oracles are speech** at every level: present, future, and passé composé
  for the past, never passé simple. That covers most of Ezekiel 4–48, all the
  Twelve except their narratives, the prayers (Daniel 9:4–19; Jonah 2;
  Habakkuk 3) and **all of Lamentations**: the poet, Zion and the man of
  chapter 3 speak, so Lamentations is in the passé composé and imparfait at L,
  B and R alike. Habakkuk 3's theophany and Jonah's psalm tell past events in
  the passé composé, as the Psalms do.
- **Narrative** is passé simple at L and B and passé composé at R, imparfait
  for repeated action: Daniel 1–6, Jonah 1; 3–4, Hosea 1, Amos 7:10–17, the
  frames of Haggai, and the dated headings. **First-person narrative** too:
  Ezekiel's visions and frames (« je vis », « il me dit » at L and B; « j’ai
  vu », « il m’a dit » at R), Daniel 7–12, Hosea 3, Amos 7:1–9; 8:1–3; 9:1,
  and Zechariah's night visions (1:7–6:8). One rendering decision in the first
  record of each block says so.
- **The prophetic perfect** follows the English level, as in Isaiah.
- **Quotation marks.** God's oracle after « La parole du SEIGNEUR me fut
  adressée : » takes none, and neither does the message the prophet is told to
  deliver (« dis-leur : Ainsi parle le Seigneur DIEU : … »). Speech quoted
  inside an unquoted oracle takes « » (Malachi's « Mais vous dites : « En quoi
  … ? » »), with “ ” only for a quotation inside that. Narrative dialogue (« Il
  me dit : « Fils d’homme, … » ») takes « » with “ ” inside, and runs across
  verses without closing at each verse (Ezekiel 44:5–46:18, Daniel's long
  angelic speeches).
- **tu / vous** follow the Hebrew's number: one person, one city or one nation
  addressed as a person is « tu »; a group is « vous ». God says « tu » to
  Ezekiel, Daniel, Jonah, Hosea and Zerubbabel; the people of Israel in the
  plural are « vous », Ephraim or Israel in the singular « tu ». When the
  Hebrew switches number inside a verse (often in Hosea and Micah), French
  switches with it.
- **Grammatical gender of personified places.** A city or land addressed as a
  woman takes feminine agreement throughout: Jerusalem, Zion and Samaria
  (Ezekiel 16; 23; Lamentations 1–2), Oholah and Oholibah, Tyre in Ezekiel
  27 (« tu as dit : Je suis d’une beauté parfaite »), Nineveh (Nahum 2–3),
  Babylon, Edom where the Hebrew is feminine. The king of Tyre (28:11–19),
  Gog and Pharaoh are masculine. Israel as God's wife in Hosea 2 is « elle »;
  Israel as God's son in Hosea 11 is « il ».

the `divine_name` span. "Lord GOD" is always `le Seigneur
{{divine_name:DIEU|Yahvé}}` (`Le Seigneur …` at a sentence start, `du
Seigneur …`, `au Seigneur …`); the span holds only DIEU.

**Units in Ezekiel 40–48.** Literal keeps « coudée », « roseau », « paume ».

by default: `{{units:environ 26,5 mètres|cinquante coudées}}`. The long cubit
is the English's 21 inches, **53 cm**, and the reed six of them, **3,20 m**;
a handbreadth is 7,5 cm, as the Pentateuch note. The alternative writes the

no-break space (U+00A0) in thousands (« 25 000 coudées »), and decimals take a
comma. Rounding: to the centimetre under 1 m, to 5 cm under 10 m, to half a
metre under 100 m, to 5 m under 1 km, then kilometres to one decimal. Use
these figures:

### Reminders for poetry-heavy prophets

- **Lines.** Where the English level has `\q`, the French level has `\q` at
  the French phrase boundary, following the Hebrew's division; most of
  Lamentations, Hosea–Malachi (Malachi at the main accent), Ezekiel 19; 27;
  32, and Daniel's hymns. Prose stays prose.
- **Typography in every line.** Typographic apostrophe ’; no-break space
  (U+00A0) inside « » and before :, narrow no-break space (U+202F) before ; !
  ? (the prophets are full of « ! » and « ? »); no line ever starts with one
  of these marks. Accents on capitals (« Ô roi », « Écoutez », « À tous »).
  Numbers in words up to *cent* in the text; units in figures.
- **Spans.** God's own first-person pronouns and possessives take
  `deity_pronoun` spans (« {{deity_pronoun:mon|Mon}} peuple », « contre
  {{deity_pronoun:moi|Moi}} »), « je » never; a possessive agrees with its noun
  (« {{deity_pronoun:ma|Ma}} fureur »). The figure on the throne in Ezekiel
  and the Ancient of Days take spans; the one like a son of man, the anointed
  one of Daniel 9, the ruler of Micah 5:1, the Branch, the angel of the LORD,
  Gabriel and Michael take none. `gender` goes on nouns only (« les
  {{gender:enfants|fils}} d’Israël »), never on « il », « celui qui »,
  « quiconque »; where the English needs a span only for a pronoun (Ezekiel 3;
  18; 33; Habakkuk 2:4), French adds none and records it once.
- **Vocatives.** No « ô » before {{divine_name:SEIGNEUR|Yahvé}}; « ô » is
  kept where the Hebrew addresses a place or thing (« ô mort » at L, « ô
  roi »).
- **Never from a reference Bible.** Daniel 3 and 6, Ezekiel 37, Jonah,
  Micah 6:8, Habakkuk 2:4 and 3:17–19, Lamentations 3:22–23, Joel 3 and
  Malachi 3–4 pull hardest toward Segond, Colombe or TOB wording: draft from
  the gloss and rewrite any line that matches for more than a short common
  phrase.
- **Decisions in English**, quoting the French; one book-wide decision at its
  first use in each job block; zero to two per sentence.

### Address, speech and settings in these books

- **God to the prophets.** God speaks to Ezekiel, Daniel (8:17), Jonah, Hosea, Amos, Habakkuk and Zechariah with *tú*. The prophets speak back to God with *tú*: Ezekiel's *¡Ah, Señor* `{{divine_name:DIOS|Yahvé}}`*!* (4:14; 9:8; 11:13; 21:5), Jonah's prayers (2; 4:2–3), Habakkuk 1 and 3, Daniel 9 and the man of Lamentations 3. *ʾAhah Adonai YHWH* follows Jeremiah 1:6: L *¡Ah, Señor DIOS!*, B *¡Ay, Señor DIOS!*, R *¡Oh no, Señor DIOS!*.
- **God to a people.** Israel, the house of Israel, the mountains of Israel (Ezekiel 6:3; 36:1–8), the priests (Malachi 1–2) and the nations are plural wherever the Hebrew is plural, so the "you" takes a `plural_you` span, verbs included: `{{plural_you:sabrán|sabréis}}`, `{{plural_you:ustedes dicen|vosotros decís}}`. Where the Hebrew is singular (Ephraim, Jacob, Israel as one person, Zion), Spanish uses *tú*. A switch from singular to plural in mid-verse follows the Hebrew and needs no decision.
- **Cities and peoples addressed as women.** Jerusalem and Samaria (Ezekiel 16; 23), Zion (Lamentations; Zephaniah 3:14; Zechariah 2:14; 9:9), Tyre as a ship (Ezekiel 27), Nineveh (Nahum 2–3) and Babylon (Zechariah 2:11) all take *tú* with feminine agreement: *estás desolada*, *eras perfecta en hermosura*. Oholah and Oholibah are *ellas*. A king or a prince addressed directly is masculine *tú*: Pharaoh (Ezekiel 29–32), the king of Tyre (28) and Gog (38–39).
- **Daniel's court.**
  - "O king" (*malka*, Hebrew *hammelekh*) is *oh rey* at L and B and *mi rey* at R, never *Majestad* (Samuel note).
  - "O king, live forever" (2:4; 3:9; 5:10; 6:7, 22): L *¡Oh rey, vive para siempre!*, B *¡Oh rey, que vivas para siempre!*, R *¡Mi rey, que vivas para siempre!*.
  - The king and his officials say *tú* to Daniel. Nebuchadnezzar addresses the three young men as a group with `plural_you` (3:14–15, 26).
  - Royal decrees "to all peoples, nations and languages" take `plural_you`: *¡Que la paz* `{{plural_you:les|os}}` *sea multiplicada!* (3:31; 6:26).
  - The Chaldeans call themselves *tus siervos*. Daniel asks the guard *Te ruego que pruebes a tus siervos* (1:12).
- **Angels.** The angel says *tú* to Daniel and to Zechariah. "Man greatly loved" (Daniel 10:11, 19) is *hombre muy amado* at L and B and *Dios te ama mucho* at R. Daniel and Zechariah call the angel *mi señor* (L, B) or *señor* (R), lowercase, with *tú* (Daniel 10:16–19; 12:8; Zechariah 1:9; 4:4–5).
- **Jonah and the sailors.**
  - The captain and the sailors say *tú* to Jonah ("What do you mean, sleeper?").
  - Among themselves they use `plural_you`: `{{plural_you:Vengan|Venid}}`*, echemos suertes* (1:7). Jonah answers them the same way: `{{plural_you:Tómenme|Tomadme}} y {{plural_you:échenme|echadme}} al mar` (1:12).
  - Their prayer to God is *tú*, with the vocative `{{divine_name:SEÑOR|Yahvé}}` and no "oh" (1:14).
  - The king of Nineveh's decree uses jussive subjunctives: *que no prueben bocado* (3:7–8).
- **Amos and Amaziah.** Both say *tú* to each other (7:12–17).
- **Lamentations.**
  - The poet speaks to Zion with feminine *tú*.
  - Zion speaks of herself in the feminine: *estoy desolada*, *me ha dejado abandonada*.
  - Zion's call to the passers-by is plural: *¿No* `{{plural_you:les|os}}` *importa nada a todos* `{{plural_you:ustedes|vosotros}}` *los que pasan por el camino?* (1:12).
  - The vocative `{{divine_name:SEÑOR|Yahvé}}` takes no "oh" (1:9, 11, 20).
- **Malachi's disputations.** God's words take no quotation marks. The people's questions take « »: *Pero* `{{plural_you:ustedes dicen|vosotros decís}}`*: «¿En qué nos has amado?»*.
- **Ezekiel's speech marks.** These follow the English note.
  - Narrative dialogue in the visions takes « »: *Y me dijo: «Hijo de hombre, ponte en pie ...»*.
  - After *La palabra del SEÑOR vino a mí:* the oracle takes no quotation marks. Neither does the message Ezekiel is told to deliver (*diles: Así dice el Señor DIOS: ...*).
  - Human speech quoted inside an oracle takes « ».
  - A speech that runs over several verses is not closed and reopened at each verse. 44:5–46:18 is one quotation, as in the English.
- **Gender spans.** These go only where the English has one and Spanish has two wordings. *Hijos*, *padres*, *hermanos*, *su* and *la persona* already include women. Hosea 2:1 is *hijos del Dios viviente*, with no span, as Romans 9:26.

### Ezekiel: other recurring words

### Lamentations

- **The acrostic.** No level imitates it in Spanish, as in English. The meaning decision at the first verse of each chapter says so. Rendering decisions never write a Hebrew letter. In decision text the letters are named in Latin script (aleph, beth ...).
- ***Ekhah*** (1:1; 2:1; 4:1). *¡Cómo ...!* at every level, as an exclamation: L *¡Cómo está sentada sola la ciudad [antes] llena de gente!*. The decision notes that it is the book's Hebrew title.
- **Lines.** Every verse is in `\q` lines at every level, mostly three per verse (*qinah*), at Spanish phrase boundaries. R may merge only where the English R does.
- **Zion's names.**
  - *la hija de Sion* (L, B), *el pueblo de Sion* (R).
  - *la virgen hija de Sion* / *de Judá* (L, B), *el querido pueblo de Sion* (R) [2 Kings 19:21 L; Isaiah 47:1].
  - *la hija de Jerusalén* (L, B), *la gente de Jerusalén* (R).
  - *la hija de mi pueblo* (L), *mi querido pueblo* (B), *mi pueblo* (R) [Jeremiah 8:21].
  - *hija de Edom* (L, B), *pueblo de Edom* (R).
- **3:22–23.** *amor leal* (L), *amor fiel* (B, R). *Raḥamim* is *compasiones* (L), *compasión* (B, R), kept apart from *ḥesed*. Do not use RV60's *misericordias* or *nunca decayeron*. *Grande [es] tu fidelidad* (L); B *¡qué grande es tu fidelidad!*; R *¡Qué fiel eres!*. `deity_pronoun` goes on *tu* / *eres* only where a free pronoun stands.
- **Voices.** Zion's first person is feminine. A change of speaker is marked by a decision only, never by quotation marks.

### Poetry reminders for Spanish

Spanish has no style guide; these come from the published Isaiah, Jeremiah and Psalms.

- Lines (`\q`) go at Spanish phrase boundaries, never between ¡ or ¿ and the word they open. A `\q` never goes inside a `{{...}}` span. B and R place their own lines and never have more than the Hebrew's cola.
- Every ¿ and ¡ has its closing mark in the same rendering, even when the question crosses a line: *¿No es así, \q casa de Israel?*.
- Narration is in the preterite. The prophetic perfect becomes a future or present, as the English level has it. Visions use L *Miré, y he aquí ...*, B *Miré y vi ...*, R *Vi ...*. *Hinneh* to a group is `{{plural_you:Miren|Mirad}}`.
- Watch the famous lines that pull toward RV60, NVI and DHH: Lamentations 3:22–23; Ezekiel 36:26; 37:1–14; Daniel 7:13–14; 12:2–3; Hosea 6:6; 11:1–4; Joel 2:13, 28; Amos 5:24; Micah 5:2; 6:8; Habakkuk 2:4; 3:17–19; Zephaniah 3:17; Zechariah 4:6; 9:9; Malachi 3:10; 4:2. Draft them from the gloss, and rewrite any line that runs with a known version for more than a short common phrase.
- Write numbers of four or more digits with a space (25 000) and decimals with a comma (3,2).

### 3. Address forms

Swahili marks singular and plural "you" (*wewe*/*ninyi*, *u-*/*m-*), so
nothing here needs a setting.

- **God to Ezekiel**: *mwana wa binadamu*, with singular imperatives
  (*simama*, *sema*, *tabiri*). God to Israel, the mountains or the shepherds:
  *ninyi*, *enyi milima ya Israeli*, *enyi wachungaji*. To a single city,
  king or nation (Tyre, Pharaoh, Gog, Jerusalem in chapter 16): *wewe*.
  Zion and Jerusalem addressed as a woman are *wewe*, and Swahili shows no
  gender.
- **Ezekiel and Daniel to God**: *Ee Bwana MUNGU* (Ezekiel); *Ee Bwana,
  Mungu mkuu na wa kutisha* (Daniel 9:4), with the vocative *Ee*. God is
  *wewe*, and spans go only on free pronouns and possessives.
- **The angel to Daniel**: *Danieli, mtu wa kupendwa sana* (L); *Danieli,
  unapendwa sana* (B); *Danieli, Mungu anakupenda sana* (R) (10:11, 19;
  9:23). The angel calls him *mwana wa binadamu* (8:17). Daniel and Zechariah
  call the angel *bwana wangu*, lowercase, at every level, and the English R
  "Sir" is the same. No span.
- **Courtiers to the king**: *Ee mfalme, uishi milele!* (L, B);
  *Mtukufu mfalme, uishi milele!* (R), as the Swahili Kings has "Your
  Majesty". Daniel to Nebuchadnezzar: *bwana wangu* (4:16). The king speaks
  to subjects as *wewe*/*ninyi*, and calls Daniel *Belteshaza* or *Danieli,
  mtumishi wa Mungu aliye hai* (6:21).
- **Royal letters** (Daniel 3:31; 6:26): *Mfalme Nebukadneza, kwa watu wote,
  mataifa na watu wa kila lugha wanaoishi duniani kote:* then *Amani yenu na
  iongezeke!* (L), *Amani iwe kwenu tele!* (B), *Nawatakia amani na
  mafanikio!* (R).
- **Jonah and the sailors**: the captain calls Jonah *wewe mwenye usingizi*
  (L) and says *Unawezaje kulala?* (B, R). The sailors speak to Jonah as
  *wewe* and to one another as *njooni* (plural), and Jonah answers them as
  *ninyi*. The sailors pray *Ee BWANA, tafadhali ...*, with the span on
  BWANA. God to Jonah: *Je, unafanya vema kukasirika?*, as *wewe*.
- **Hosea**: God to Hosea is *wewe* (*Nenda, ujitwalie mke*). God speaking
  of Israel as a wife uses *yeye*, which needs no gender handling.
- **Malachi**: God to the priests and people is *ninyi*. Their objections
  are in double quotes, and their *u-* to God takes no span.

### 4. Reminders from the Swahili style guide for poetry-heavy prophets

- **Lines**: where an English level has `\q`, the Swahili level has the same
  breaks, following the Hebrew division and placed at Swahili phrase
  boundaries. Do not split a verb from its object marker or a noun from its
  agreeing adjective.
- **Tense**: the oracles are not narrative. Use the future *-ta-*, the
  present *-na-*/*-a-* or the perfect *-me-* as the Hebrew's sense asks. Use
  the narrative *-li-* then *-ka-* only in the prose stories (Daniel 1–6,
  Jonah, the dated sections of Ezekiel, Haggai and Hosea 1 and 3). The
  formula *asema BWANA* stays in the *-a-* form.
- **Speech**: oracles and God's first-person speech take no quotation
  marks. Speech quoted inside an oracle takes “ ”, and a quotation inside
  that takes ‘ ’. Store all of them straight in `content/`. Narrative
  dialogue in Ezekiel's visions and in Daniel takes quotation marks, as prose
  does.
- **Settings**: `deity_pronoun` goes only on free pronouns and possessives
  (*mimi*, *wangu*, *yake*, *nami*, *kwangu* ...), never on a verb prefix or
  an object marker. Count fewer spans than the English. Spans go on the
  figure on the throne (Ezekiel 1:26–28; 8; 10; 43), the Ancient of Days and
  Zephaniah 3:17. None go on the angel of the LORD, the man in linen, the
  "one like a son of man", the anointed one of Daniel 9, the ruler of Micah
  5:1, Zerubbabel or the Branch himself. A word *inside* a translated name
  (*Si Watu Wangu*) takes no span. Swahili has almost no `gender` spans, so
  where the English has one (Ezekiel 3 and 18 case law, Micah 6:8,
  Habakkuk 2:4, Malachi 3:24), Swahili's *mtu*, *mwanadamu*, *wana*,
  *watoto* and *-ake* already cover both sexes, and the drafter writes no
  span. A decision is needed only at the first such place in a job. The
  English open question on generic "he" in Ezekiel 3 and 18 therefore does
  not affect Swahili. `units` spans appear only where the English has them.
- **Literal**: words Swahili needs that the Hebrew lacks go in [brackets],
  for example *[ni]*. A subject prefix or object marker is never bracketed.
- **Spelling**: write *ng'* with the apostrophe (*ng'ambo*, *kung'aa*). In
  running text numbers are in words (*watu mia moja na ishirini*), while
  measures, units and Daniel 6:2's "120 satraps" follow the published
  blocks: figures from 100 up.
- **Independence**: draft from the Hebrew, the Aramaic and the gloss. The
  Union Version pulls hardest on Ezekiel 34, 36:25–27 and 37:1–14, Daniel
  3, 6 and 7:9–14, Hosea 11, Joel 3:1–5, Amos 5:24, Jonah 2, Micah 4:1–4,
  5:1 and 6:8, Habakkuk 2:4 and 3:17–19, Zechariah 4:6 and 9:9, Malachi 3,
  and Lamentations 3:22–26. Rewrite any line that matches it for more than a
  short common phrase.
- **Reuse**: published Swahili is reused at each level where the Hebrew is
  the same, with a decision for each difference:
  - Obadiah 1–9 reuses Jeremiah 49:9–16.
  - Micah 4:1–3 reuses Isaiah 2:2–4, and Micah 3:12 reuses Jeremiah 26:18.
  - Nahum 2:1 reuses Isaiah 52:7.
  - Joel 2:13, Jonah 4:2 and Nahum 1:3 reuse Exodus 34:6.
  - Habakkuk 2:14 reuses Isaiah 11:9.
  - Jonah 2 reuses the published Psalms where it quotes them.
  - Joel 4:16 is the same as Amos 1:2.
- **Decisions** are in English and quote the Swahili, with zero to two per
  sentence. Each choice above is recorded once, at its first use in each job.
- **Status** stays `ai_draft`.

### 3. Address

These forms add to the Isaiah note: God says *ngươi* (L) / *con* (B, R) to
one person in commissions and promises, and *ngươi* at every level in
rebukes.

- **God to Ezekiel**: *Hỡi con người*, then *ngươi* (L) / *con* (B, R).
  Ezekiel calls himself *tôi* in the narrative. To God he says *con*, with
  *Ngài* and *Lạy Chúa là CHÚA*. The figure on the throne and the voice
  from the temple (43:6) are God.
- **The guide of chapters 40–48** (the bronze man) and **the angels of
  Daniel and Zechariah** say *tôi* and *ông* at every level, as Gabriel does
  in Luke 1:13, 19 ✓. After *Hỡi con người* the pronoun may be dropped:
  *Hỡi con người, hãy nhìn cho kỹ.* Daniel and Zechariah say *thưa chúa
  tôi* for *'adoni* (L, B; R *thưa ngài*, uncertain), as Joshua 5:14. They
  call themselves *tôi*, or *đầy tớ của chúa tôi* where the Hebrew says "your
  servant" (Daniel 10:17; L, B). The angel's "Daniel, greatly loved" (10:11,
  19) is *Hỡi Đa-ni-ên, người rất được yêu quý*. None of these take a deity
  span.
- **God to Hosea, Amos, Jonah, Habakkuk, Zechariah**: *ngươi* (L) / *con*
  (B, R). This includes God's questions to Jonah in chapter 4 and to Amos in
  7:8 and 8:2. Each prophet says *con* to God and calls him *Ngài*. "O LORD"
  is *lạy CHÚA* with no "O". "Please, LORD" (Jonah 4:2) is *Ôi, lạy CHÚA*.
- **God to Israel, Ephraim, Jerusalem and the nations**: *ngươi* / *các
  ngươi* in rebuke at every level. In the tender passages (Hosea 2:16–25;
  11:8–9; 14:5–9; Zephaniah 3:14–20) it is *con* (B, R). Malachi's
  disputations are *các ngươi* throughout.
- **Kings and courtiers in Daniel**: subjects say *Hỡi vua* (L) / *tâu bệ
  hạ* (B) / *thưa đức vua* (R) for "O king". They call themselves *các đầy tớ
  của vua* (L, B) / *chúng tôi* (R), and *tôi* when one man speaks for
  himself. Nebuchadnezzar and Belshazzar say *ta* to everyone, with *ngươi* /
  *các ngươi* to Daniel, his friends and the wise men. Darius speaks to
  Daniel with *ông* (6:17, 21), out of respect and concern (uncertain). The
  queen speaks to Belshazzar as a subject (*tâu bệ hạ* at B). The three
  friends answer Nebuchadnezzar with *Tâu vua* and *chúng tôi* (3:16–18).
  Daniel answers the king with *tôi* and *vua* / *bệ hạ* / *đức vua*. Nobody
  in Daniel uses *tao*, *mày* or *bọn bay*.
- **Jonah and the sailors**: the captain and the sailors say *ông* to Jonah
  and *chúng tôi* for themselves. Jonah says *tôi* and *các ông*. The
  sailors praying to the LORD (1:14) say *Ôi, lạy CHÚA* and *chúng con*.
  The king of Nineveh's decree is impersonal (*người và súc vật …*), with
  *ta* only where the king speaks.
- **Amaziah and Amos** (Amos 7:10–17): they say *ông* to each other at
  every level, and Amaziah's contempt is carried by *hỡi kẻ tiên kiến*. Amos
  says *tôi*.
- **Micah, Malachi and Zechariah speaking for God to the people**: the
  oracle's *các ngươi*. Where a prophet speaks in his own voice he says
  *tôi* and *anh em* when he pleads, as in the Isaiah note. "Mortal" in
  Micah 6:8 is *ngươi* (L, B) / *anh em* (R) (uncertain).
- **Lamentations**: as the table above. The poet and Zion say *tôi*; the
  community says *chúng con*; God is *Ngài*.

### 4. Reminders from the style guide for poetry-heavy prophets

- Translate from the Hebrew and Aramaic with the gloss and the meaning
  decisions. Never copy Bản Dịch 2011, Bản Phổ Thông, Bản Dịch Mới or any
  Catholic version. The 1926 wording ("Hỡi con người", "Đấng Thượng Cổ")
  is public domain, but it is archaic: keep only short fixed phrases from
  it, and rewrite any line that runs on with it for more than a short
  common phrase. This matters most in Ezekiel 37, Daniel 7, Joel 2–3,
  Micah 6:8, Habakkuk 3, Zechariah 9 and Lamentations 3.
- **Lines**: where an English level has `\q` breaks, the same Vietnamese
  level has them, at Vietnamese phrase boundaries, following the Hebrew
  division. A verse always starts a line. Where a Hebrew sentence runs
  across verses, close each Vietnamese verse as its own sentence and record
  it once per block (Psalms rule). Ezekiel's and Daniel's prose stays prose.
- **Speech**: God's oracles and the prophet's poetry take no quotation
  marks. Speech inside an oracle and narrative dialogue take straight double
  quotes, and single quotes for a quotation inside a quotation. Speech is
  introduced with a colon (`Ngài phán với tôi: "…"`).
- **Spans**: `divine_name` on every YHWH, *Chúa là CHÚA* for *Adonai YHWH*,
  and the span on CHÚA only in *CHÚA Vạn Quân*. `deity_pronoun` goes on
  *Ta* and *Ngài* mid-sentence, never on a name's syllable (*Ta-rê-si*, *Không
  Phải Dân Ta*) and never on the Ancient of Days' companion "one like a son
  of man", the anointed one of Daniel 9, the ruler of Micah 5, the Branch,
  the angel of the LORD, or the guide of Ezekiel 40–48. There is no `gender`
  span unless Vietnamese really has two wordings: *người*, *con cái*, *ai*
  and *mình* already cover both. `units` spans go only where the English has
  them (section 1).
- **Pronouns for the people**: *chúng* for the guilty, the nations and
  enemies in the oracles; *họ* in promises and neutral narration, as in
  Jeremiah. A city or nation is *nó*. Zion and the women of Ezekiel 16 and 23
  are *nàng* in the third person.
- **Readable**: short sentences and plain words. Use *sứ điệp* over *sấm
  ngôn* and *người cai trị* over *thủ lĩnh*, and explain images where the
  English R does: the hand of the LORD, set your face, horns. Do not add
  explanations the English R lacks.
- **Brackets** are for Literal only: supplied words in `[ ]`. *Đã*,
  *đang* and *sẽ* are added only where the sense needs them, unbracketed.
- **Numbers**: in words in running text (*bảy mươi năm*, *hai mươi mốt
  ngày*); figures from 100 (*120 tổng trấn*, *1.290 ngày*, *2.300 buổi chiều
  và buổi sáng*); measures in figures with a decimal comma. Spelling as
  the style guide: *hòa, thủy, khỏe*, stored in NFC.
- **Rendering decisions** are in English, quoting the Vietnamese, zero to
  two per sentence. A book-wide choice from this sheet is recorded once, in
  the first file of each job. "Son of man" (Ezekiel 2:1 or a job's first
  chapter), *Chúa là CHÚA*, the "you will know" refrain, the units at 40:5,
  the Hosea names and *Than ôi* each need one.
- *Sử Ký* stays only the title of Chronicles. The "book" Daniel is told to
  seal is *quyển sách* (L, B) / *cuộn sách* (R where the English says
  scroll), never *Sử Ký*.

### 0. One new rule: particles after `{{divine_name:하나님|야훼}}`

"Lord GOD" (*Adonai YHWH*) is `주 {{divine_name:하나님|야훼}}`, as before. In Ezekiel it comes about 200 times. 하나님 ends in a consonant and 야훼 in a vowel, so a particle after the span has to read correctly after both.

- **Allowed:** 께서, 께, 의, 도, 만, 에게, 인 (as in 내가 주 {{divine_name:하나님|야훼}}인 줄), and 이다 written in full (주 {{divine_name:하나님|야훼}}이다).
- **Never:** 이/가, 은/는, 을/를, 과/와, (으)로, and the vocative 이여/여. As a vocative, use the bare name: "아, 주 {{divine_name:하나님|야훼}}!" [Jer 1:6; Judg 6:22].
- `{{divine_name:주|야훼}}` is not affected, because both forms end in a vowel. Its particles stay as before (가, 를, 는, 여).

This is why "declares the Lord GOD" at B and R below is no longer written the Isaiah way (…가 말한다).

**Lamentations**

- **Acrostic.** No Korean acrostic and no letter headings in the text: the shared meaning decisions carry it, and any letter names stay in the English decision text. Each verse keeps its own content (one letter each, three in chapter 3), so never move a clause across a verse boundary in these chapters.
- ***'Ekhah*** (1:1; 2:1; 4:1). L and B: 어찌하여 … 가! ([그 성이] 어찌하여 홀로 앉아 있는가!). R: 슬프다! followed by a plain sentence [Isa 1:21, which is *'ekhah* in all three]. The decision names it as the book's Hebrew title.
- **Daughters.** Daughter Zion is 딸 시온 (L, B) and 시온 백성 (R). Virgin daughter Judah is 처녀 딸 유다 (L, B) and 유다 백성 (R) [2 Kings 19:21]. The daughter of my people is 내 백성의 딸 / 사랑하는 내 백성 / 내 백성 [Jer 8:21]. Daughter Edom is 딸 에돔 / 딸 에돔 / 에돔 백성. Uz is 우스 [Gen].
- **Voices.** Zion's words to passers-by are plain 해라체 with 나. When she turns to God (1:9c, 11c, 20–22; 2:20–22) they are 합쇼체 with 저 and {{divine_name:주|야훼}}여.
  - The man of chapter 3 speaks *about* God in the plain style with -시- (주님께서 나를 … 하셨다). From 3:22–23 ("your faithfulness") and from 3:42 on he speaks *to* God in 합쇼체 (주님의 신실하심이 크십니다; 저희가 … 주님께서는 용서하지 않으셨습니다).
  - Chapter 5 is all communal prayer: 합쇼체 with 저희.
  - Follow the Hebrew's turns verse by verse, as Psalm 23. No quotation marks.
- **3:22–23.** *Hesed* is 한결같은 사랑 (L, B) and 변함없는 사랑 (R). Mercies (*rachamim*) are 긍휼 (L, B) and 자비 (R), kept distinct. Faithfulness is 신실하심 [Ps]. Draft from the gloss: the 개역 and 새번역 lines are very familiar.
- **Zion as a woman.** Never 그녀: ISA and JER have none. Repeat 시온 or 예루살렘, or use 그 성, or 그 여자 at L where a pronoun is unavoidable [Jer 4:31], or leave the subject unsaid. R's 시온 백성 takes 그들.
- Lamentations has no *ne'um* formulas. *Adonai* (2:1–7; 3:31–58) is 주님 and YHWH is the span: keep them apart.

  - The Pleiades and Orion (Amos 5:8) are 묘성 and 삼성 (L, B) and 별 무리와 오리온자리 (R) (uncertain).

### 3. Address forms new in these books

- **God to Ezekiel, Daniel, Jonah, Habakkuk and the others.** 해라체 with 너, and the vocative 사람의 아들아. God never takes -시- in his own mouth.
  - **The narrator about God.** Ezekiel narrates in the first person in the plain style and gives God -시-: 그분께서 나에게 말씀하셨다. The same holds for Daniel 7–12, Amos, Habakkuk, Zechariah and Jonah's narrator.
  - **The guide in Ezekiel 40–48** ("the man") takes no -시-: 그 사람이 나에게 말했다. Switch to -시- only where the English marks God speaking with a deity span (43:6–7; 44:2, 5).
- **Prophets to God.** 합쇼체 with 저, and 저희 when they pray for the people.
  - Ezekiel's cry: 아, 주 {{divine_name:하나님|야훼}}! [Jer 1:6]. "Lord GOD, you know" (37:3) is 주 {{divine_name:하나님|야훼}}, 주님께서 아십니다.
  - Jonah keeps 합쇼체 even in his anger (4:2–3, 9). Habakkuk's complaints are 합쇼체. Daniel 9's prayer is 합쇼체 with 저희, addressing 주님 and {{divine_name:주|야훼}}여, and "the great and awesome God" is 크고 두려우신 하나님.
  - In Habakkuk 3 and Jonah 2, the speech level follows each verse's turn between speaking to God and speaking about him, as in the Psalms.
- **Ezekiel and the people.** The exiles ask him in 합쇼체 with 당신 (12:9; 21:12; 24:19; 37:18). Ezekiel answers in his own voice in 하오체 with 여러분 [Jer 42:4, 9]. The oracle he passes on stays 해라체 (uncertain).
- **Daniel and the court.**
  - Courtiers, Daniel and his friends to a king: 합쇼체 with 임금님 and 저/저희. The three refuse in 합쇼체 too (3:16–18).
  - Kings to Daniel and to subjects: 해라체 with 너 (다니엘아; 살아 계신 하나님의 종 다니엘아, 6:21). The queen mother to Belshazzar: 합쇼체.
  - Decrees and letters (3:31–4:34; 6:26–28): 해라체. Nebuchadnezzar's first-person account in chapter 4 is in the plain style with 나.
  - The chief official and the steward to the young men: 해라체; Daniel to them: 합쇼체 (1:10–13). Daniel to Arioch: 합쇼체.
- **Angels and Daniel; the angel and Zechariah.**
  - The angel (Gabriel, the man in linen, the angel who talked with me) speaks 해라체. "Man greatly loved" is 크게 사랑받는 사람 다니엘아 / 하나님께서 귀하게 여기시는 사람 다니엘아 / 하나님께서 몹시 아끼시는 다니엘아.
  - Daniel and Zechariah answer in 합쇼체 and address the angel as 내 주여 (*adoni*), never 주님, which is kept for God. This holds at every level (uncertain).
  - The angel of the LORD prays in 합쇼체: 만군의 {{divine_name:주|야훼}}여, … 언제까지 … 않으시겠습니까? (Zech 1:12).
- **Jonah and the sailors.** Strangers speak 합쇼체 to each other with 당신, as the Genesis note does for strangers. That covers the captain's rebuke (1:6), the sailors' questions (1:8) and Jonah's answers ("저는 히브리 사람입니다"). The sailors' prayer is 합쇼체 to {{divine_name:주|야훼}}. The king of Nineveh's decree is 해라체.
- **Amos and Amaziah (7:10–17).** Amaziah's report to Jeroboam is 합쇼체. To Amos he speaks hostile 해라체 (선견자야, 가거라). Amos answers in 해라체, and God's words inside his answer are 해라체 (uncertain; cf. Jer 28:15).
- **Hosea and Gomer (3:1–3).** 해라체 at L, and 해요체 with 당신 at B and R [Genesis note, husband and wife] (uncertain).
- **Haggai and the priests (2:11–14).** Haggai asks in 하오체, and the priests answer in 합쇼체 (아닙니다) (uncertain).
- **Malachi.** The people and the priests speak to God in 합쇼체 with 저희, and God answers in 해라체.

### 4. Style points for the poetry-heavy prophets

- **Lines.** Put `\q` where the English level has it, at that level, placing the Korean breaks yourself. A verse start is always a line start. Where a Hebrew sentence runs across verses, close each verse as its own Korean sentence [Psalms settled].
- **Oracles.** No quotation marks around God's speech or the prophet's poetry. Speech inside an oracle takes " ", and ' ' when nested. "Thus says the LORD" ends in a period.
- **Imperatives and endings.** Modern 해라체 imperatives (들어라, 울부짖어라, 보아라); -겠다 and -ㄹ 것이다 for the future.
  - Never 개역's -니라, -노니, -지어다, -리라, -찌어다, -하라 in proclamations, or 할지어다.
  - The texts that pull hardest are Ezekiel 37, Daniel 7 and 12, Hosea 6 and 11, Joel 2–3, Amos 5:24, Jonah 2, Micah 5:1 and 6:8, Habakkuk 2:4 and 3:17–18, Zechariah 4:6 and 9:9, Malachi 3–4 and Lamentations 3:22–23. Draft them from the gloss and rewrite any line that matches 개역개정 or 새번역 beyond a short common phrase.
- **Honorifics follow the English deity spans.** God and his Spirit take -시-, 께서 and 그분 in narration and in human speech.
  - God never takes -시- in his own mouth.
  - No -시- for the figures the English leaves without a span: one like a son of man, the anointed one of Dan 9:25, the ruler of Mic 5:1, the Branch, the king of Zech 9:9, "my messenger", the shepherd of Zech 13:7, the guide of Ezekiel 40–48 and the angels.
  - Korean has no deity_pronoun spans.
- **The divine name.** Every YHWH is the span, with the particle outside it. A bare 주 never stands for YHWH; 주님 is *Adonai*, and 여호와 is never used. For the particles after `{{divine_name:하나님|야훼}}`, see section 0.
- **Hangul only.** No Latin letters (MENE, the acrostic letters) and no Hebrew or Chinese characters. Letter names and Hebrew words belong only in the English decision text, transliterated.
- **Readable.** Short sentences and native words (자비 rather than 긍휼, 분노 rather than 진노). R explains idioms where the English R does, with a decision where the English has one.
  - R does not turn 시온 백성 back into a woman.
  - R keeps 사람의 아들아, 의의 해, 다림줄 and 셀라: images the English keeps at every level are not explained away.
- **Numbers.** Years, ages and large numbers are in figures: 390일, 120명의 태수, 2,300 저녁과 아침, 1,290일. Months and days stay in words. Under 100, counts are in words in running text (사십 일, 예순두 번의 일곱).
- **Rendering decisions.** Zero to two per sentence. Record each book-wide choice above once, in the first file of your job, as an English-language decision quoting the Korean.
