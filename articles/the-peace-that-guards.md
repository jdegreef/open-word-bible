Philippians 4:6–7 is one of the most loved passages in Paul's letters, and one of the most quoted to anxious people. Read closely, it says more than "don't worry". Here is the Balanced level:

> {{OWB:PHP.4.6.s1:B}}

## "Do not worry"

The verb {{GREEK:PHP.4.6:2}} is anxious care, the same word Jesus uses in Matthew 6 ("do not worry about your life"). Paul can use it in a good sense: in 2:20 it describes Timothy's genuine concern for the church. Here the context makes it worry, the kind that crowds God out.

## Prayer, and specific requests

Paul uses two words for prayer. The first is prayer of every kind. The second is asking for something particular. Together they mean: bring everything to God, including the specifics. The Readable level says it plainly: "{{OWB:PHP.4.6.s1:R}}"

## Thanks with the asking

The thanksgiving goes with the requests. It is not something to offer later, once the answer has come. That is a small grammatical point with a large consequence for how we pray.

## A promise, not a payment

Verse 7 begins with "and". What follows is the result of bringing everything to God, not a reward for praying well. God's peace is promised, not earned.

## Beyond understanding

The phrase about understanding can mean that God's peace is beyond what the mind can grasp, or that it achieves more than any human planning could. Both fit. The translation follows the more common reading, marks the decision uncertain, and notes the other.

## A garrison

The verb in verse 7, {{GREEK:PHP.4.7:10}}, is what a garrison does. Philippi was a Roman colony, and retired soldiers were among its citizens. Paul's readers would have known what it looked like when soldiers stood guard over a city. So the Balanced level says God's peace will "stand guard over" your hearts and minds, and the Readable level that it will "keep watch over" them. Both keep the military picture without the older, vaguer "keep".

In biblical language the heart is the centre of the whole person, and the second word is a thought or plan. Heart and mind together mean the whole inner life.
