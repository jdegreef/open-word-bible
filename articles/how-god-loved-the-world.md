Many English readers know John 3:16 by heart, in the words "For God so loved the world". Open Word Bible's Balanced level reads a little differently:

> {{OWB:JHN.3.16.s1:B}}

The change is deliberate, and the record for the verse explains it. Every decision about the meaning of the verse is shared by every language; the English wording follows from them.

## "So" or "in this way"?

The verse opens with {{GREEK:JHN.3.16:1}}. In English, "God so loved the world" is usually heard as "God loved the world so much". But the Greek word normally means "thus" or "in this way", and that is how John uses it elsewhere: the wind blows where it wills, and "so" is everyone born of the Spirit (3:8); Jesus, tired from the journey, sat down "thus" by the well (4:6).

It has also just appeared two verses earlier: as Moses lifted up the snake in the wilderness, "so must the Son of Man be lifted up" (3:14). Verse 16 begins with "For", which ties it to that sentence. Read together, they say: this is how God loved the world, by giving the Son to be lifted up. 1 John 4:9 says the same thing in other words: "in this the love of God was revealed, that God sent his one and only Son".

So the Literal level says it plainly:

> {{OWB:JHN.3.16.s1:L}}

## Why the decision is marked uncertain

The other reading has real support. The size of the love is plain from what it gave, and readers have long heard "so loved" as "loved so much". The record marks the decision uncertain and gives the familiar wording in a footnote.

That is why Balanced says "God's love for the world was such that". In English, "such" can mean "of this kind" or "so great". It keeps the question open, where "so much" would close it in one direction. It does depart from the familiar wording, and the rendering decision says so.

Readable spells out the "how":

> {{OWB:JHN.3.16.s1:R}}

## The rest of the verse

A few more decisions in the record are worth knowing:

- **The world** is humanity as a whole, the world that did not know its maker (1:10) and loves darkness (3:19). That God loves *this* world is the surprise.
- **One and only** translates {{GREEK:JHN.3.16:12}}, the word older Bibles render "only begotten". It means unique; see ["One and only" or "only begotten"?](/articles/one-and-only-son/). A footnote gives the older wording.
- **Gave** includes both sending the Son into the world (verse 17) and giving him up to death, the lifting up of verse 14.
- **Perish** is the opposite of eternal life: final ruin, not physical death, since believers still die (11:25). Readable says "be lost", the sense the same verb has in 6:39 and 10:28.

The record also notes a possible echo of Genesis 22, where God tells Abraham to take "your son, your only son, whom you love". Here God gives his own Son. John does not draw the link himself, so this decision too is marked uncertain.

## Where does Jesus stop speaking?

One more uncertain decision concerns the whole paragraph. The manuscripts have no quotation marks, so no one knows for sure where Jesus's words to Nicodemus end. Some think they end at verse 15, and that verses 16 to 21 are John's own reflection. Open Word Bible keeps the quotation open to the end of verse 21, since nothing in the text marks a break, and gives the other view in a footnote.
