There are already many good English Bibles. So why start another one?

Two reasons. The first is **permission**. The second is **reasoning**.

## Permission

Almost every modern translation is copyrighted. That is not a scandal; translation is expensive, and copyright is how the cost is usually paid. But it means that anyone who wants to quote a translation at length, print it, put it in an app, record it, or build a new resource on it has to ask first, and is often told no or asked to pay.

The limits are real. The publisher of one of the most widely used English Bibles lets anyone quote up to 500 verses without asking, as long as the verses do not make up a whole book of the Bible or a quarter of the new work. That is generous for a sermon or a study guide. It is not enough for a Bible app, a reading plan that covers a year, an audio recording, or a translation into another language that wants to follow it closely. Even the King James Version, four centuries old, is still under a royal patent in the United Kingdom, administered by Cambridge University Press.

Older translations are free in most countries, and a few modern ones have been placed in the public domain, such as the World English Bible. Open Word Bible stands in that tradition. Churches in poorer countries, small app makers, teachers and translators into other languages feel the cost of permission most, and they are the people a free text helps most.

## Reasoning

Every translation makes thousands of choices. Does a Greek word mean "overcome" or "understand"? Is a phrase "only begotten" or "one and only"? Should "brothers" include the sisters in the room? A good translation team argues about these for hours. Then the reader receives the result and almost none of the argument. A few footnotes survive; the rest is invisible.

Take John 1:5. The King James Version says the darkness "comprehended it not". The World English Bible says the darkness "hasn't overcome it". A reader with both open sees two Bibles disagree and has no way to know why. The Greek verb, {{GREEK:JHN.1.5:13}}, can mean to seize and overpower, or to grasp with the mind. Open Word Bible reads:

> {{OWB:JHN.1.5.s1:B}}

The record beside the verse says why. John uses the same verb in 12:35 of darkness overtaking someone, which supports "overcome". The record also says that John may intend both senses, marks the decision *uncertain*, and gives the other reading in a footnote. You do not have to take our word for it. The evidence is on the page.

## What Open Word Bible does differently

**Everything is free to use.** The translation, its notes and its data are released under [CC0](https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/), which places them in the public domain. You may quote it, print it, adapt it, put it in software, or sell a book that contains it, without asking.

**The work is shown.** Every sentence carries a *reasoning record*: the original words, a word-for-word gloss, the translation at three reading levels, and each decision made along the way, with the alternatives that were considered and the reason for the choice. You can open any verse on this site and read them. The article [Showing our work](/articles/showing-the-work/) explains how.

**It is translated from the original languages.** Every language is translated from the Hebrew, Aramaic and Greek, not from English. A Spanish sentence starts from the same decisions about meaning as the English one and makes its own choices about wording.

**It is honest about where it stands.** Everything on this site today is AI-assisted. It is not marked reviewed until named reviewers who read Hebrew or Greek have checked it, and every sentence's status is recorded in the open data. See [AI-assisted translation and human review](/articles/ai-assisted-translation-and-human-review/).

## Fair questions

**Can a translation drafted with AI be trusted?** Not on its own word, and we do not ask you to. That is why every decision is written down where anyone can check it, why the software rejects work that breaks the project's rules, and why no sentence is called reviewed until a named person who reads the original has approved it. A hidden decision cannot be checked. A written one can.

**Does showing every choice make the Bible look less certain?** It shows where the real questions are, and how few of them change anything a reader would build on. Most decisions are about wording. Where the meaning itself is a close call, other Bibles have usually made the same call quietly. We would rather you knew.

**If anyone may change it, what stops a distorted copy?** Nothing in law, just as nothing stops someone printing an altered King James Version in most of the world today. What protects readers is that the original records stay public, with their full history, so any copy can be compared with them.

## Where we are

The project is young. Books from both Testaments are being drafted in English, and Spanish, French, Swahili, Vietnamese and Korean follow, each from the Greek and Hebrew. Every sentence comes with its full reasoning, and the [Read](/read/) page shows what is translated so far. It is there to show the method and to be criticised. If you read Greek or Hebrew, or simply notice something that reads badly, the [project on GitHub](https://github.com/jdegreef/open-word-bible) is the place to say so.
