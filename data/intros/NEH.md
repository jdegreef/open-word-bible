## What the book is

Much of Nehemiah is written in the first person, the memoir of a man of action and of prayer. He is cupbearer to King Artaxerxes in Susa when news comes that the walls of Jerusalem are broken down and its gates burned. He weeps, fasts and prays, and when the king asks what is wrong, he prays again in the moment before he answers:

In Jerusalem he inspects the walls by night and calls the people to build. Every family takes a section. Sanballat, Tobiah and Geshem mock, threaten and plot, so the builders work with a tool in one hand and a weapon in the other, and the wall is finished in fifty-two days. Nehemiah also faces trouble within: the rich are lending at interest and taking the poor's children as slaves, and he makes them give it back.

Then Ezra reads the law aloud to all the people from morning until noon, and the Levites explain it. The people weep, and are told to celebrate instead, for "the joy of the LORD is your stronghold". They keep the Feast of Booths, confess their sins in a long prayer that retells Israel's history, and sign a covenant to keep the law. The book ends with Nehemiah's second term as governor, when he finds the covenant already broken and puts things right again, praying each time, "Remember me, my God, for good."

## Who wrote it, and when

Nehemiah came to Jerusalem in "the twentieth year of King Artaxerxes" (2:1), Artaxerxes I, in 445 BC, and governed for twelve years before going back to the king and returning later (13:6–7). Ezra and Nehemiah were one book in the Hebrew Bible. Its writers wove Nehemiah's own memoir together with lists, records and the account of Ezra's reading of the law, probably in the fourth century BC.

## How the book is laid out

- **Nehemiah's prayer and his journey** (1–2).
- **Rebuilding the wall** (3–4): the builders, the mockers and the guard.
- **Justice for the poor** (5).
- **The wall finished** (6–7): the plots against Nehemiah, and the list of those who returned.
- **The law read and the covenant renewed** (8–10): the Feast of Booths and the great prayer.
- **Jerusalem repopulated and the wall dedicated** (11–12).
- **Nehemiah's reforms** (13).

## How Open Word Bible translates Nehemiah

**Verse numbers.** The verses are numbered as in the Hebrew text, which differs from most English Bibles in three places: Hebrew 3:33–38 is their 4:1–6, so all of Hebrew chapter 4 is six verses higher there; Hebrew 7:68–72 is their 7:69–73; and Hebrew 10:1 is their 9:38. Notes at those verses explain.

**Read clearly (8:8).** The law was read "clearly, and they gave the sense". The Hebrew word can mean distinctly, or with explanation section by section, and Jewish tradition took it to mean that the reading was translated into Aramaic as it went. The draft says "clearly", marks the decision uncertain and footnotes the others.

**Your stronghold (8:10).** The familiar rendering is "the joy of the LORD is your strength". The word means a stronghold, a place to flee to for safety, and the draft keeps that picture: joy in God's goodness shelters the people when the law has made them grieve. A footnote gives "your strength".

**Who prays the great prayer? (9:6).** In the Hebrew the prayer follows the Levites' call with no new speaker, so it reads as their prayer on the people's behalf; the Greek translation adds "And Ezra said". The draft follows the Hebrew and footnotes the Greek.

**Horses and mules (7:68).** The list of those who returned repeats Ezra 2, but the Hebrew text here goes straight from the singers to the camels, without the line about horses and mules that Ezra 2:66 has. A copyist's eye may have jumped from one 245 to the next. The draft follows its Hebrew text and footnotes the line.

**Building words (3).** Chapter 3 names the gates, towers and sections of the wall, and some of its words are uncertain. The decisions give the most likely sense of each and mark them uncertain.

**The divine name.** The text writes God's personal name "the LORD" in small capitals, and a reader setting shows "Yahweh" instead. Nehemiah often prays to "the God of heaven". See [the divine name](/terms/the-divine-name-yhwh/).
