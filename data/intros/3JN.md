## What the book is

3 John is the shortest book in the Bible by number of words, a private letter like thousands that have survived from the ancient world. "The elder" writes to his "dear friend Gaius", glad to hear that he is walking in the truth. Gaius has welcomed travelling believers who set out "for the sake of the Name", though they were strangers to him, and the elder asks him to send them on their way well supplied:

> {{OWB:3JN.1.8.s1:B}}

Not everyone does the same. Diotrephes, "who loves to be first", refuses to receive the elder's messengers, spreads wicked talk, and drives out of the church those who want to welcome them. The elder warns that he will deal with it when he comes, commends Demetrius, and closes, as in 2 John, with the hope of speaking face to face.

## Who wrote it, and when

The writer, as in 2 John, calls himself only "the elder", and the two letters share their style and their closing words. Like 1 and 2 John, the letter was credited from early times to John the apostle; many scholars think instead that the elder was a leader of the communities that treasured John's Gospel. It is usually dated around AD 90–110. Gaius, Diotrephes and Demetrius are known only from this letter.

## How the book is laid out

- **Greeting and prayer** (1–4): to Gaius, whom the elder loves in truth.
- **Welcome for travelling believers** (5–8): become "fellow workers with the truth".
- **Diotrephes** (9–10): who refuses to receive us.
- **Do what is good, and Demetrius** (11–12).
- **Closing** (13–15): greet each of the friends by name.

## How Open Word Bible translates 3 John

**In truth (1).** The elder loves Gaius "in truth". The words can mean simply "truly", but the letter uses "truth" six times for the truth of the gospel that believers hold and walk in, and the draft keeps both senses together. The decision is marked uncertain.

**The Name (7).** The travellers set out "for the sake of the Name". With no further word, "the Name" stands for the one who bears it, most likely Jesus Christ, as when the apostles are glad "to bear shame for the sake of the Name" in Acts 5:41; in Jewish use it could also stand for God. The decision is marked uncertain.

**Fellow workers with the truth (8).** This can mean working with the missionaries for the truth, or working alongside the truth itself, as if the truth were at work in the world. The draft keeps the wording open, marks the decision uncertain, and footnotes the readings.

**The letter we do not have (9).** The elder says he "wrote a few lines to the church". The letter has not survived, unless it is one we know; some have thought of 2 John, but that letter says nothing about hospitality or Diotrephes. Some manuscripts read "I would have written", perhaps because copyists were uneasy that a letter of the elder had been lost. The draft follows its Greek text and footnotes the others.

**Joy, or grace? (4).** "No joy of mine is greater than this": a few manuscripts read "grace" here, a word that differs by one letter in Greek. The draft follows its Greek text and footnotes the other. See [When manuscripts disagree](/articles/when-manuscripts-disagree/).

**Brothers and sisters.** The travelling believers are "brothers" in the Greek, a word that can include women. The draft uses a reader setting for it. See ["Brothers" or "brothers and sisters"?](/articles/brothers-and-sisters/).
