## What the book is

2 Thessalonians is a second letter to the young church in Thessalonica, and it follows on from the first. The believers are still suffering persecution, and Paul encourages them: God will put things right when the Lord Jesus is revealed from heaven. But something has unsettled them. A prophetic word, a spoken message or a letter claiming to come from Paul has told them that the day of the Lord has already come. Paul answers that it has not:

> {{OWB:2TH.2.3.s1:B}}

Some of the believers have stopped working, perhaps because they think the end has come, and are living off others. Paul reminds them of his own example and of the instruction he gave them when he was with them: "Anyone who will not work should not eat." He ends by signing the letter in his own hand, "the sign of genuineness in every letter".

## Who wrote it, and when

The letter is from "Paul, Silvanus and Timothy" (1:1), like 1 Thessalonians. If Paul wrote it, it was sent from Corinth around AD 50–51, a few months after the first letter. Many scholars accept it as his. Others think a follower wrote it in his name later, pointing to how closely it copies the outline and phrases of 1 Thessalonians while differing in tone, and to its different picture of the end: in the first letter "the Lord's day comes like a thief at night", while here certain events must come first. Those who hold it to be Paul's reply that he is answering a new misunderstanding.

## How the book is laid out

- **Greeting and thanks** (1:1–4): for their growing faith and love under persecution.
- **The justice of God** (1:5–12): when the Lord Jesus is revealed from heaven.
- **The day of the Lord has not come** (2:1–12): the rebellion, the man of lawlessness, and what is holding him back.
- **Stand firm** (2:13–17): God chose you to be saved.
- **Pray for us** (3:1–5).
- **The idle and disorderly** (3:6–15): anyone who will not work should not eat.
- **Closing** (3:16–18): greeting in Paul's own hand.

## How Open Word Bible translates 2 Thessalonians

**Already here (2:2).** The false claim was not that the day of the Lord was near, which Paul himself taught, but that it had already arrived. The Greek verb means that it has come and is present, and the draft says "already here". The footnote notes that some translations read "is at hand".

**What is holding him back (2:6–7).** Paul says the Thessalonians know what is restraining the lawless one, and he does not explain it. In verse 6 the Greek word is neuter, "what is holding him back", and in verse 7 masculine, "the one who now holds it back". Readers have suggested the Roman empire and its emperor, Paul's own preaching of the gospel, an angel such as Michael, the Holy Spirit, or God himself. The draft keeps the wording as open as the Greek, marks the decision uncertain and footnotes the readings.

**God's temple (2:4).** The man of lawlessness "takes his seat in God's temple". The temple in Jerusalem was still standing when the letter was written, and Paul may mean it; or the words may picture a claim to God's own place, or speak of the church as God's temple, as in 1 Corinthians 3:16. The decision is marked uncertain and footnoted.

**The first of a harvest (2:13).** God chose the Thessalonians "as the first of a harvest" to be saved. Other manuscripts read "from the beginning". In the oldest manuscripts, written in capitals, the two readings differ by a single letter, and both have good support. The draft follows its Greek text, marks the decision uncertain and footnotes the other. See [When manuscripts disagree](/articles/when-manuscripts-disagree/).

**The evil one (3:3).** The Lord will "guard you from the evil one": the Greek can also mean "from evil", as in the Lord's Prayer (Matthew 6:13). The draft takes it as Satan, since Paul has spoken of Satan's work in 2:9, and footnotes the other reading.

**Brothers and sisters.** Paul calls the believers "brothers", a word that in Greek can include women. The draft uses a reader setting for it. See ["Brothers" or "brothers and sisters"?](/articles/brothers-and-sisters/).
