## What the book is

2 Timothy reads as a last letter. Paul is in chains in Rome, deserted by many, and he expects to die soon. He writes to Timothy, his "dear son", remembering his tears and the sincere faith that lived first in his grandmother Lois and his mother Eunice. He urges him not to be ashamed of the gospel, to guard the good treasure entrusted to him, to endure hardship like a soldier, an athlete and a farmer, and to keep away from quarrels about words. Hard times are coming, but Timothy has Paul's example and the Scriptures he has known since childhood. And Paul looks back on his own life:

The letter ends with news of friends who have left and friends who have stayed, and with a request: come before winter, and bring the cloak, the scrolls and the parchments.

## Who wrote it, and when

The letter is from Paul (1:1), and the church accepted it as his from the second century. As with 1 Timothy and Titus, many scholars today think instead that a follower wrote it in his name after his death. Of the three, 2 Timothy is the one most often thought to be Paul's own, for its many personal details. If he wrote it, it comes from his last imprisonment in Rome, in the mid-60s, shortly before his death under Nero.

## How the book is laid out

- **Greeting and thanks** (1:1–7): the faith of Lois and Eunice, and "stir up into flame the gift God gave you".
- **Do not be ashamed** (1:8–18): guard the good treasure; deserted by many, refreshed by Onesiphorus.
- **A good soldier of Christ Jesus** (2:1–13): endure hardship, and a trustworthy saying.
- **A worker approved by God** (2:14–26): handle the message of truth correctly, and avoid foolish arguments.
- **Terrible times in the last days** (3:1–9).
- **Continue in what you have learned** (3:10–17): all Scripture is breathed out by God.
- **Preach the word** (4:1–8): the race finished and the crown of righteousness.
- **Personal news and final greetings** (4:9–22).

## How Open Word Bible translates 2 Timothy

**Breathed out by God (3:16).** Paul writes that "all Scripture is breathed out by God and is useful". The sentence has no verb in the Greek, and most supply "is" before "breathed out by God", as the draft does. Some read the word as describing the subject instead: "every Scripture breathed out by God is also useful". The decision is marked uncertain and the footnote gives that reading.

**What I have entrusted (1:12).** Paul is sure God "is able to guard what I have entrusted to him until that day". The Greek says "my deposit", which may be what Paul has entrusted to God, his life and work, or the gospel God has entrusted to Paul, the "good treasure" of verse 14, which is the same word. The decision is marked uncertain and the footnote gives the second reading.

**He remains faithful (2:13).** The trustworthy saying of 2:11–13 breaks its own pattern in the last line: "If we are unfaithful, he remains faithful, for he cannot be false to himself." Most read this as assurance; some as a warning that Christ stays true to his word, including the warning of verse 12. The decision is marked uncertain and footnoted.

**Captured to do his will (2:26).** The verse uses two different words for "him", which may point to different persons. The draft reads both as the devil, who "has held them captive to do his will", and footnotes the other readings.

**The lion's mouth (4:17).** "I was rescued from the lion's mouth" recalls Psalm 22 and Daniel 6. A decision reads it as a picture of mortal danger, not of the arena or of Nero, and marks it uncertain.

**Deity pronouns.** Pronouns for God and for Jesus are written in lower case; a reader setting capitalizes them.
