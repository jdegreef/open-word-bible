## What the book is

Joel begins with a disaster everyone could see. Swarm after swarm of locusts has stripped the land: the vines and fig trees are bare, the grain is ruined, and there is nothing left even for the offerings in the temple. Joel calls the elders, the farmers, the drinkers of wine and the priests to mourn. Then the locusts become an army, sweeping over the land like dawn spreading over the mountains, and the prophet sees in them the day of the LORD. Yet it is not too late:

> {{OWB:JOL.2.13.s1:B}}

The LORD answers his people's prayer. He will drive the invader away and make up for "the years that the swarming locust has eaten". And afterward comes a greater promise, the one the apostle Peter quoted on the day of Pentecost (Acts 2:16–21):

> {{OWB:JOL.3.1.s1:B}}

The book ends with the nations gathered for judgment in the valley of Jehoshaphat, "the valley of decision", and with Judah and Jerusalem secure, the mountains dripping with sweet wine and a spring flowing from the house of the LORD.

## Who wrote it, and when

Nothing is known of Joel son of Pethuel beyond the first verse, and the book gives no date. It names no king, and speaks of priests and elders, of the temple and its offerings, and of the Greeks as traders in slaves (4:6). Many scholars date it after the exile, around 500–350 BC, when Judah had no king and the second temple stood. Others have placed it much earlier, in the reign of King Joash in the ninth century BC, from its place among the earlier prophets. The book shares many phrases with other prophets, among them Amos, Isaiah and Obadiah.

## How the book is laid out

- **The plague of locusts** (1): mourn, all of you, for the land is ruined.
- **The day of the LORD and the call to return** (2:1–17): the invading army, and "tear your hearts, not your clothes".
- **The LORD answers** (2:18–27): the invader driven away, and the years the locusts ate restored.
- **The Spirit poured out** (3): signs in the sky, and "all who call on the name of the LORD will be saved".
- **Judgment of the nations and blessing for Judah** (4): the valley of decision.

## How Open Word Bible translates Joel

**Verse numbers.** The verses are numbered as in the Hebrew text, which has four chapters. Chapter 3 here, the promise of the Spirit, is 2:28–32 in most English Bibles, and chapter 4 here is their chapter 3. Notes at 3:1 and 4:1 explain.

**Four names for locusts (1:4).** Joel names four locusts: the cutting, the swarming, the hopping and the stripping locust. They may be four kinds of locust, four stages in its growth, or simply four names for one plague. The draft keeps four names, marks the decision uncertain and footnotes the readings.

**Locusts or an army? (2).** The invader of chapter 2 is described as an army, and called "the northern army" in 2:20. The decisions take it as the locust swarm pictured as an invading people, as Proverbs 30:27 calls locusts a people, and footnote the reading that a human army is meant, foreshadowed by the locusts.

**The turning point (2:18).** "Then the LORD became jealous for his land": the verbs are the ones Hebrew uses to tell what happened next, so the people have done what the prophet asked and God has answered. Some read them as a promise still to come. The decision is marked uncertain and footnoted.

**The day of the LORD.** Joel speaks of it more than any other prophet but Zephaniah. A decision at 1:15 explains the phrase: the day when the LORD comes to act in judgment, and to rescue.

**The divine name.** The text writes God's personal name "the LORD" in small capitals, and a reader setting shows "Yahweh" instead. See [the divine name](/terms/the-divine-name-yhwh/).
